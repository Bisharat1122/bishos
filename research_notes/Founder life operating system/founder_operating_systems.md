# Founder Operating Systems & Time Management for Early-Stage, Near-Solo Co-Founders

Scope: a co-founder with almost no team who personally does client/project delivery, sales and marketing. These notes collect concrete mechanics (stages, quotas, cadences, templates) to turn into modules in a personal life OS, with a company layer later.

Source-quality note for the report writer: primary sources were reached for Paul Graham, Strategic Coach (podcast transcript), EOS Worldwide (Level 10, Rocks, Process Documenter, Delegate & Elevate), Pete Kazanjy's *Founding Sales* (Holloway), Lenny's Newsletter (Jen Abel episode page) and Farnam Street. Dan Martell, Hormozi, Justin Welsh, Dan Koe, Scaling Up and the Porter/Nohria CEO study came mostly from secondary summaries, interviews or excerpts. Those are flagged inline.

## 1. How should an early-stage founder split the week between delivery, sales, marketing and company building? Are there recommended allocations or example schedules?

### Takeaway
No source gives an evidence-based percentage split for a near-solo founder. The best-supported structure is to (a) protect half-day maker blocks for delivery and deep work, (b) group meetings and calls into set windows or themed days, and (c) give customer acquisition about half of total effort (Weinberg's "50% rule"), letting the pipeline shift the ratio. Theme days (Dorsey) and Free/Focus/Buffer days (Sullivan) are the two concrete patterns for doing this at week level.

### Cited Findings

**Maker's vs manager's schedule (Paul Graham)**
- The manager's schedule divides the day into one-hour slots. The maker's schedule uses blocks of "units of half a day at least". An hour is "barely enough time to get started." — [Paul Graham, Maker's Schedule, Manager's Schedule](http://www.paulgraham.com/makersschedule.html)
- "A single meeting can blow a whole afternoon, by breaking it into two pieces each too small to do anything hard in." For a maker, a meeting is "like throwing an exception." Graham notes that even expecting a meeting later can stop someone starting ambitious work in the morning. — [Paul Graham](http://www.paulgraham.com/makersschedule.html)
- "Speculative" meetings such as coffee chats cost little on a manager's schedule but are "terribly costly" on a maker's. — [Paul Graham](http://www.paulgraham.com/makersschedule.html)
- Graham's tactics: hold office hours, i.e. set blocks for meetings; put meetings at the end of the day ("Because they come at the end of my day these meetings are never an interruption"); historically he programmed at night and did business by day. "Y Combinator runs on the maker's schedule." — [Paul Graham](http://www.paulgraham.com/makersschedule.html)

**Theme days (Jack Dorsey, 2011, while running both Twitter and Square)**
- Mon = management meetings and "running the company"; Tue = product; Wed = marketing, communications and growth; Thu = developers and partnerships; Fri = company and culture; Sat = hiking; Sun = reflection, feedback and strategy. His reasoning: "There's interruptions all the time, but I can quickly deal with an interruption and know 'it's Tuesday, I have product meetings, I have to focus on product stuff.'" — [CNN Money, Dorsey at Techonomy 2011](https://money.cnn.com/2011/11/13/technology/dorsey_techonomy/)
- Secondary sources say Dorsey later said he kept this routine only for a limited time. — [Ongig blog](https://blog.ongig.com/career-advice/jack-dorsey-weekly-schedule/) (secondary, unverified)

**Free / Focus / Buffer Days (Dan Sullivan, Strategic Coach "Entrepreneurial Time System")**
- Free Day: "a 24-hour period from midnight to midnight that has no work-related activity". No "computers, no cell phone, no emails" and no business reading. Its purpose is rejuvenation. "Just don't have an unplanned Free Day." — [Strategic Coach podcast transcript](https://charm.strategiccoach.com/resources/podcasts/inside-strategic-coach/135794272)
- "Focus Days are for profitability." They are "Front Stage" days. Buffer Days are in-between days for preparing future business activity and getting ready for Free and Focus Days. A co-host suggests a Buffer Day before and after a week-long vacation, and a Focus Day before a long run of Free Days. — [Strategic Coach podcast transcript](https://charm.strategiccoach.com/resources/podcasts/inside-strategic-coach/135794272)
- Sullivan on structure: "You gravitate to the part of your life that has the most structure", and "Structure means that I'll be supported by things that are already planned." — [Strategic Coach podcast transcript](https://charm.strategiccoach.com/resources/podcasts/inside-strategic-coach/135794272)
- A secondary handout defines a Focus Day as at least 80% of time spent on your top three revenue-generating activities, and Buffer Days as "Back Stage" work: building processes, training, delegating, clearing paperwork. — [Kresser Institute handout](https://my.kresserinstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/BB-Balance-Productivity-Tip.pdf) (secondary; the 80% figure is not in Sullivan's own words in the transcript)

**Recommended selling vs building splits (heuristics only)**
- Weinberg and Mares (*Traction*), "50% rule": spend about half your effort on traction/customer acquisition and half on product, from day one, not after the product is "done". — [Startup Grind, The 50% Rule](https://startupgrind.com/blog/the-50-rule); [Graham Mann book notes](https://grahammann.net/book-notes/traction-gabriel-weinberg). One reviewer calls the 50 split "a starting point to argue with, not a law." — [echai.ventures summary](https://echai.ventures/startingup/from/the-founders-tribune-excerpt-from-the-book-traction)
- A vendor blog suggests about 60% demand creation and 40% closing for founder-led motions, then shifting toward sales as meetings come in ("let the pipeline dictate the mix"). — [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/startup-marketing-vs-sales) (opinion)
- "Sell first, build your product later". Founders who polish for months under-validate. — [Rho blog](https://www.rho.co/blog/founder-takes-focus-on-selling-not-building) (opinion)
- YC/Michael Seibel lists common first-time-founder mistakes, including not launching and "not knowing where your first users will come from." — [YC blog](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/the-biggest-mistakes-first-time-founders-make/)

**CEO time-allocation audits (Porter & Nohria, HBR 2018, "How CEOs Manage Time")**
- 27 CEOs, about 60,000 hours of data. CEOs logged their time around the clock for three months. — [CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2018/10/25/success/ceos-time-management); [McKinsey/HBR award blog](https://www.mckinsey.com/about-us/new-at-mckinsey-blog/how-do-ceos-manage-their-time-the-winners-of-the-60th-annual-hbr-mckinsey-award-explain)
- Reported figures (secondary coverage, could not verify against HBR directly): about 62.5 working hours a week; 9.7 hours per weekday, 3.9 per weekend day, 2.4 per vacation day; 28% of work time alone, mostly in blocks of an hour or less; 6.9 hours sleep. — [MGMA summary](https://www.mgma.com/articles/test-of-time-harvard-study-reveals-how-ceos-spend-their-days-on-and-off-work); [CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2018/10/25/success/ceos-time-management)
- 61% of work time face-to-face, 15% phone/written correspondence, 24% electronic. — [CEO Update](https://www.ceoupdate.com/how-ceos-manage-priorities-allocate-their-time-and-energy) (single secondary source)
- First Round Review (Bill Trenchard, "70% of Time Could Be Used Better", per a secondary listing): block one or two hours on your top three priorities when you are sharpest; batch reactive work such as email; end meetings with a decided outcome. — [echai.ventures listing](https://echai.ventures/startingup/productivity-time/how-do-i-track-where-my-hours-actually-go-so-i-know-what-to-cut-without-turning-tracking-into-another-chore) (attribution unverified)

### Inferences
- For a co-founder who also does delivery, the useful unit is the half-day block, not the hour. A practical template: mornings = maker blocks (client delivery and content writing); afternoons = manager blocks (sales calls, client calls, admin), with calls grouped into "office hours".
- Theme days fit a near-solo founder only partly. Delivery deadlines and prospect availability rarely fit one day a week. A hybrid works better: one or two themed days (e.g., a Sales/Outreach day and a Buffer/Admin day) plus a daily recurring sales block. Dorsey's main benefit, knowing what a given interruption belongs to, can be had by tagging every block with a domain.
- App implications: (1) blocks typed as maker or manager; (2) day types Focus/Buffer/Free, with Free Days planned first and protected; (3) a target-vs-actual allocation dashboard by domain (Delivery / Sales / Marketing / Company-building / Admin / Recovery); (4) a "meeting cost" warning when a meeting would split a maker block.
- Reasonable starting allocation for a services-heavy co-founder (inference, no source): about 40–50% delivery, 25–30% sales, 10–15% marketing/content, 10% company-building/admin. Re-weight weekly based on pipeline coverage versus delivery load.

### Gaps
- Found no rigorous study of how early-stage (pre-seed/seed, under 5 people) founders actually split their time. All percentages are heuristics from practitioners or vendors.
- Could not fetch the HBR Porter/Nohria article itself. Category breakdowns (e.g., % on strategy vs people) are not confirmed here.
- Found no documented weekly calendar from a named services-plus-product co-founder.

## 2. What does a minimum viable founder-led sales system look like (stages, activities, metrics, weekly rhythm)? Which activity metrics matter most?

### Takeaway
Use 5–7 stages with clear exit criteria. Every open deal needs a dated next step. Run a daily follow-up block and a weekly pipeline review. Track a small set of input metrics (new outreach per day, conversations/demos booked per week) and output metrics (win rate, time in stage, stalled deals). Hormozi's "Rule of 100", 100 primary actions a day, is the best-known quota, but it assumes about 4 hours a day, which is a lot for someone who also delivers.

### Cited Findings

**Stages and exit criteria**
- Kazanjy: stages need "crisp definitions" that mean the same thing to everyone; generic CRM stages and close percentages are "pretty silly" early on. His TalentBin stages: Demo Scheduled → Qualified → Seeking Approval → Sent Proposal → Negotiation → Verbal Agreement → Contract Sent → Closed-Won / Closed-Lost / Closed-Unqualified. Closed-Unqualified means the account never had the need, so the demo should never have happened. — [Founding Sales, Pipeline Management (Holloway)](https://holloway.com/g/founding-sales/sections/pipeline-management)
- A lean founder version: start with five stages (Lead → Qualified → Demo → Proposal → Closed) and add stages only when a real deal needs one, because every extra stage is a box someone has to keep updating. — [Cut the SaaS guide](https://cut-the-saas.com/guides/how-to-build-a-sales-pipeline)
- Give each stage an exit rule. E.g., discovery is done when pain, budget and decision-maker are confirmed. Disqualify quickly rather than keep weak deals alive. — [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/founder-led-sales-early-stage-startups)
- Jen Abel (enterprise): outreach/qualification → discovery call → demo/proposal → co-authoring the scope of work → procurement → signature. — [Lenny's Newsletter, Jen Abel](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/master-founder-led-sales-jen-abel)
- Kazanjy's three stages of founder sales overall: proto-sales (discovery), founder-led (repeatability), founder-led+ (delegation). — [leadmagic.io summary of Founding Sales](https://leadmagic.io/gtm-skills/founder-sales/raw) (secondary)

**Next-step discipline and rhythm**
- "Every stage needs an explicit next step... with a set date and time." Pipelines fall apart when deals have no next step, or have one with no scheduled time. — [Holloway / Kazanjy](https://holloway.com/g/founding-sales/sections/pipeline-management)
- Cadence: a weekly pipeline review (Kazanjy blocked 2 hours late Wednesday afternoon) plus a "floating hour a day" for pipeline follow-up. Once you know your carrying capacity, cap new top-of-funnel intake (e.g., "three net-new demos a day"). — [Holloway / Kazanjy](https://holloway.com/g/founding-sales/sections/pipeline-management)
- Flags: no activity in 7 days on a down-funnel deal; about 2 weeks stuck in one stage; about 9 days in "Contract Sent" should be investigated. Track "uncovered" deals with no future meeting on the calendar. — [Holloway / Kazanjy](https://holloway.com/g/founding-sales/sections/pipeline-management)
- Benchmarks from his example: a solo seller doing all prospecting runs about "15 [demos] a week, and 60 a month"; about 20% close rate at about $5K ACV; SDRs book 5–10 demos a week at about 25% close per demo. — [Holloway / Kazanjy](https://holloway.com/g/founding-sales/sections/pipeline-management)
- Book the next call before hanging up. If the prospect won't schedule it, that is a red flag; "email you later" often means no interest. — [Lenny's Newsletter, Jen Abel](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/master-founder-led-sales-jen-abel)

**Outreach mechanics**
- Jen Abel: outreach should be 3–4 sentences, relevant to the prospect's role, lead with a counterintuitive insight and the problem rather than the solution. The test is whether the reader thinks "That's interesting." Channels: cold email, LinkedIn DMs, cold calls (calls can get much higher response rates in some cases). Avoid demos on the first call. Early founder sales are about learning, not revenue. — [Lenny's Newsletter, Jen Abel](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/master-founder-led-sales-jen-abel)
- Tyler Bosmeny (Clever, YC "How to Start a Startup"): funnel = Prospecting → Conversations → Closing → Revenue. Early adopters are a small share of the market (cites Rogers' 2.5% "innovators"). He contacted 400+ companies in the first two months of YC. Founders should sell; passion and industry knowledge matter more than sales experience. — [Startup Grind write-up of Bosmeny talk](https://startupgrind.com/blog/how-to-learn-to-sell-startups-sales-101-by-tyler-bosmeny-ceo-at-clever)

**Activity quotas: Hormozi's Rule of 100**
- Hormozi describes the rule as 100 primary actions a day: e.g., 100 minutes of content, 100 outreach attempts, or $100/day on ads. He says doing it takes most people "about 4 hours a day ish". — [Modern Wisdom #830 transcript](https://podcasts.happyscribe.com/modern-wisdom/830-alex-hormozi-24-controversial-truths-about-success-failure)
- Advanced version, "open to goal": work until you hit an outcome target, not an input count. — [Modern Wisdom #830 transcript](https://podcasts.happyscribe.com/modern-wisdom/830-alex-hormozi-24-controversial-truths-about-success-failure)
- *$100M Leads* "core four" ways to get attention: warm outreach, cold outreach, free content, paid ads. — [Shortform summary](https://www.shortform.com/summary/100m-leads-summary-alex-hormozi) (secondary)
- The claim that you should keep at it for 100 days before judging appears only on a third-party page. — [claudeskills.info page](https://claudeskills.info/skills/getagentseal/founder-playbook/100m-leads/) (unverified)

**Metrics**
- Early on, watch win rate over raw conversion. A higher win rate (e.g., 30%) means fewer leads needed; a lower one means more volume, with quality still the main driver. Qualification is "the biggest opportunity for improvement" in most processes. — [Lenny's Newsletter, Jen Abel](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/master-founder-led-sales-jen-abel)
- Per-deal fields Kazanjy relies on: stage, explicit next step with date/time, time in current stage, deal value, date of last activity, whether a future meeting exists. — [Holloway / Kazanjy](https://holloway.com/g/founding-sales/sections/pipeline-management)
- A suggested trigger to hire the first rep: once the founder has closed at least ~10 customers and spends about 20% of their time on sales. — [Qwilr blog](https://qwilr.com/blog/founder-led-sales/) (vendor opinion)

### Inferences
- **Minimum viable sales module** (synthesis):
  - Stages: Target → Contacted → Conversation booked → Discovery done (pain/budget/decider confirmed) → Proposal sent → Verbal yes → Won / Lost / Unqualified.
  - Required fields: next step and next-step date (block saving without them), value, source channel, last-touch date, owner.
  - Automatic flags: deal with no future step; 7 days idle; 14 days in one stage; proposal or contract open 9+ days.
- **Daily**: one fixed outreach block (e.g., 60–90 min) with a numeric quota, plus a follow-up block driven by "next step due today". **Weekly**: a pipeline review of 1–2 hours (Kazanjy uses Wednesday; Friday fits a solo founder's review day too).
- **Core metrics** (lead indicators first): new outreach sent/day; replies; conversations booked/week; proposals sent/week. Then win rate, average deal size, sales cycle length, and pipeline coverage (open weighted value vs revenue target).
- Use Rule of 100 as a configurable quota. Let the user pick "primary action" units (DMs, calls, minutes of content, ad dollars) and a daily target that fits their time (e.g., 20–30 touches/day when delivering full time), plus an "open to goal" mode (stop when N meetings are booked).
- The same pipeline fits client delivery: once a deal is Won, it becomes a project with commitments (see Q4). Keep a single Contact/Company record shared by the sales pipeline and the personal CRM.

### Gaps
- No primary source gave a validated multi-touch follow-up cadence (e.g., day 1/3/7/14) for founder outreach. Widely repeated stats such as "80% of sales need 5+ follow-ups" were not found in a reliable source, so they are not included.
- Could not read the full Jen Abel transcript (pricing, design partners, follow-up counts, hiring triggers were not on the page).
- Could not confirm Hormozi's exact book wording for the Rule of 100.

## 3. What does a minimum viable founder-led marketing/content system look like when time is scarce?

### Takeaway
Practitioners who run as one-person businesses converge on one pattern: one long-form "hub" piece a week (a newsletter), cut into 6–30 short "spokes" for social, made in fixed time slots with templates and an idea bank. Reported time budgets run from about 30–45 minutes a day to about 2 hours a day, or about 10 hours a week. Hormozi's equivalent quota is "100 minutes of content a day".

### Cited Findings
- Justin Welsh's Content OS sales page describes a multi-step system for a high-quality newsletter plus "6–12 pieces of high-performance social media content each week." — [Justin Welsh, Content OS](https://learn.justinwelsh.me/content)
- Secondary write-ups describe Welsh's flow: the newsletter is the hub; spokes are story posts, data-backed observations, contrarian takes and listicles. About an hour of ideation/research stored in Notion, then drafting on a four-step template. A 10-step pipeline runs from idea capture through newsletter, pre/post CTAs and a thread breaking down the newsletter. — [Vic Montano Substack](https://thevicmontano.substack.com/p/content-os-by-justin-welsh-an-automated); [Mango Network Substack](https://themangonetwork.substack.com/p/the-creator-operating-system) (secondary)
- One write-up describes a six-week distribution plan for each idea: teaser + newsletter (week 1), thread (week 2), LinkedIn carousel (week 3), short-form (week 4), resurface with a fresh angle (weeks 5–6). — [Email Breakdown](https://emailbreakdown.beehiiv.com/p/how-jw-built-a-9-1m-solo-biz) (secondary; attribution among write-ups is uncertain)
- Welsh reportedly works about 10 hours a week using SOPs, Airtable content dashboards and automations, with no full-time employees and one part-time VA, and reports $12.5M+ lifetime revenue. — [Built by Foundry case study](https://www.builtbyfoundry.io/blog/justin-welsh-solopreneur-12m-empire) (secondary, self-reported figures)
- A talk summary says Welsh treats daily writing "as a job" and built "systems, operations, and templates" to save time. He started by writing daily about SaaS for about 7 months before leaving his job to consult. — [Typeshare post](https://typeshare.co/geoffdecker/posts/how-daily-writing-on-linkedin-can-build-your-audience) (secondary)
- Dan Koe: one weekly newsletter is repurposed into a Medium post, YouTube script and podcast episode. He gathers ideas for a day or two, then outlines as hook → insight → practical process. — [Tagabukid Substack](https://tagabukid.substack.com/p/how-dan-koe-writes-one-newsletter) (secondary). Reported writing time is about "2 hours a day". — [Trends.vc](https://trends.vc/how-does-dan-koe-get-millions-of-views-writing-just-2-hours-a-day/); [beehiiv blog](https://blog.beehiiv.com/p/how-dan-koe-makes-2-million-per-year-writing-2-hours-per-day). Other sources give 30–45 minutes, so the figures conflict.
- Hormozi: "100 minutes of content" a day is one valid Rule-of-100 unit; content is one of the "core four" lead channels. — [Modern Wisdom #830 transcript](https://podcasts.happyscribe.com/modern-wisdom/830-alex-hormozi-24-controversial-truths-about-success-failure); [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/summary/100m-leads-summary-alex-hormozi)
- Graham's maker-schedule logic applies to writing: writers, like programmers, need half-day blocks. — [Paul Graham](http://www.paulgraham.com/makersschedule.html)

### Inferences
- **Minimum viable content module** (synthesis):
  - **Idea bank**: capture inbox tagged by pillar and format; sales conversations and client delivery feed it, since every objection or FAQ is a post.
  - **Weekly hub**: one fixed maker block (2–3 hours), e.g., Tuesday or Thursday morning, to write one long-form piece.
  - **Spokes**: one batching block (60–90 min) to cut the hub into 5–10 short posts using fixed templates (story, contrarian, listicle, data/observation, how-to).
  - **Daily engagement**: a short manager-type slot (15–30 min) for posting, comments and DMs, which doubles as warm outreach for sales.
  - **Recycling**: queue hub ideas to resurface after N weeks.
- Total budget for a near-solo founder: about 4–6 hours a week (hub 2–3 h, spokes 1–1.5 h, engagement 15–30 min/day). That is well below Welsh's ~10 h and Hormozi's ~100 min/day, and appropriate while delivery is heavy.
- Track content inputs (pieces published per week vs target, streak) and outputs that link to sales (inbound DMs, leads tagged "source: content"). That closes the loop with the Q2 pipeline's source field.

### Gaps
- Found no verified, first-person daily or weekly schedule from Welsh, Koe or Hormozi. Time figures come from secondary write-ups and conflict.
- No reliable data comparing outcomes of different content time budgets for B2B service founders.

## 4. How should a founder track commitments to clients, partners and co-founders so nothing falls through the cracks? (incl. personal CRM and decision logs)

### Takeaway
Combine three established patterns: (1) a GTD-style "Waiting For" list for things others owe you, plus a matching list of promises you made, each with an owner, a date and a check-again date; (2) a weekly review that sweeps both; (3) EOS-style 7-day To-Dos for promises made in meetings, with a ~90% weekly completion target. Add a cadence-based personal CRM (contact frequency tags → "due" list) and a decision journal written before outcomes are known.

### Cited Findings

**Waiting-for and weekly review (GTD)**
- A Waiting For list records "things you need from someone or something else: a reply, a review, a delivery, or a decision". Note what you're waiting for, who or what it depends on, and when you'll check again. — [Super Productivity, GTD Waiting For](https://super-productivity.com/blog/gtd-waiting-for-list/) (secondary paraphrase of Allen)
- The weekly review checks the Waiting For list ("What's the current status of delegated actions? Follow up... if necessary"), sends reminders for overdue items and deletes entries you no longer care about. — [Super Productivity, Weekly Review](https://super-productivity.com/blog/gtd-weekly-review-guide/); [Forte Labs, The Weekly Review is an Operating System](https://fortelabs.com/blog/the-weekly-review-is-an-operating-system/)

**EOS To-Dos and Issues as commitment trackers**
- In the Level 10 meeting, a To-Do is a 7-day action item that is either done or not done. It shouldn't stay on the list more than two weeks, and about 90% should come off each week. — [EOS Worldwide, Level 10 Meeting](https://www.eosworldwide.com/blog/level-10-meeting)
- Off-track numbers, off-track Rocks, and concerns from headlines go on an Issues List, which is worked through by IDS (Identify, Discuss, Solve). Solving an issue usually produces To-Dos. — [EOS Worldwide, Level 10 Meeting](https://www.eosworldwide.com/blog/level-10-meeting)

**Sales and client next steps as commitments**
- Every deal needs an explicit, calendared next step. Calendared next actions mean follow-ups are "scheduled rather than remembered." — [Holloway / Kazanjy](https://holloway.com/g/founding-sales/sections/pipeline-management)
- Be clear about what you do and don't do; don't oversell. Customers who feel they were sold to quickly tend to churn. — [Lenny's Newsletter, Jen Abel](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/master-founder-led-sales-jen-abel)

**Personal CRM (relationship cadence)**
- A Notion personal-CRM template maps frequency tags to days (weekly = 7, monthly = 30, quarterly = 90, semesterly = 182, yearly = 365) and shows people who are "due" based on last-contact date. — [valentin_pd Intentional Relationship template](https://valentinperez.notion.site/Intentional-Relationship-Aid-Template-df40dfccf65b41b48dfce4391ef2817b)
- The Obsidian Personal CRM plugin infers last contact from dated notes that link a person, so the only manual input is a cadence per person. — [Obsidian Personal CRM plugin](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/personal-crm)
- A tiered example from relationship-driven sales: personal contact with top referrers every 30 days; in-person or phone no more than 90 days apart; email monthly. — [iXact Contact](https://ixactcontact.com/?p=3758) (real-estate context)

**Decision logs (Farnam Street decision journal)**
- Fields: situation/context; problem statement or frame; variables that govern the situation; complications; alternatives seriously considered and why they were rejected; range of outcomes; expected outcome with reasoning and "actual probabilities you assign to each projected outcome" ("The degree of confidence matters, a lot."); time of day and how you feel physically and mentally. Optional: second- and third-order effects, worst case, opportunity cost, what the decision looks like in 5 weeks, 5 months or 5 years. — [Farnam Street, Decision Journal](https://fs.blog/decision-journal/)
- Write the entry before acting ("looking in this mirror before you act, not after") so hindsight bias can't rewrite your reasoning. — [Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/decision-journal/)
- Secondary templates add a 1–10 confidence score and a scheduled review weeks later comparing prediction with reality. — [MyLifeNote blog](https://blog.mylifenote.ai/decision-journal/) (secondary)

### Inferences
- **Commitments module** (synthesis): one object, "Commitment", with fields direction (I owe / owed to me), counterparty (contact, client, co-founder), source (meeting, email, sales call, project), promised date, check-again date, status, and link to the project/deal.
  - Quick capture from any meeting note ("I'll send the proposal by Fri" → I-owe; "they'll send the brief" → Waiting-for).
  - Every Commitment shows up in Today when due and is swept in the Friday review. Overdue I-owe items surface first, since broken promises to clients cost the most.
  - Report a weekly "promise-keeping rate", in the spirit of EOS's ~90% To-Do drop-off.
- Co-founder commitments: run a lightweight weekly co-founder sync using the L10 skeleton (scorecard numbers, Rock status, To-Do review, issues list) so cross-founder promises live in one shared list. This is also the seed of the later company layer.
- Personal CRM: contacts carry a relationship tier (e.g., inner circle = 14–30 days, clients/partners = 30 days, warm network = 90 days, wider = 182–365 days). Last-contact is auto-updated from logged interactions, meetings and sales activities, and a "due to reconnect" list appears in the weekly plan. Shared with the sales pipeline so a prospect who goes cold becomes a nurture contact rather than vanishing.
- Decision log: a short form with the eight Farnam fields (most optional), a confidence %, and an automatic review reminder (e.g., 30/90 days) to record what actually happened. It can be linked to EOS-style Issues once the company layer exists.

### Gaps
- Found no source specifically on co-founder commitment tracking; the design above is synthesis.
- No primary David Allen text was retrieved; GTD wording is via secondary guides.
- Found no founder-specific evidence on optimal relationship cadences; the tiers are borrowed from generic and sales sources.

## 5. How do you get "delegation-ready" before having a team?

### Takeaway
Run a two-week time-and-energy audit, score each task by value and energy (Martell's DRIP, or EOS's Delegate & Elevate), work out a Buyback Rate to see what is worth handing off, and document processes as you do them: record the screen (Camcorder Method), then turn the recording into a short checklist. Martell's Replacement Ladder sets the order: admin first, then delivery, marketing, sales, leadership. EOS's Process Documenter keeps each SOP to the 20% of steps that produce 80% of results.

### Cited Findings

**Time and Energy Audit (Martell)**
- Log what you do every 15 minutes for two weeks ("Every 15 minutes, stop and write down the task you're doing"). Label each task with $–$$$$ value (e.g., $ ≈ $20/h, $$$$ ≈ $100+/h). Mark each task energizing or draining (green/red). — [Shortform, Time Audit](https://www.shortform.com/blog/time-audit/); [Rise with Drew](https://risewithdrew.com/mastering-your-time-and-energy-dan-martells-proven-audit-method-to-reclaim-focus-and-productivity/) (secondary)
- The Buyback Loop: Audit ("Identify low-value tasks that are sucking your time and energy") → Transfer (to someone better at it who enjoys it) → Fill (with higher-value, energizing work). — [Readingraphics summary](https://readingraphics.com/book-summary-buy-back-your-time/) (secondary)

**DRIP Matrix (Martell)**
- Quadrants by money generated × energy: Delegation (low money, drains energy), Replacement (high money, drains energy), Investment (low money, gives energy), Production (high money, gives energy, the target quadrant). — [SuperSummary](https://www.supersummary.com/buy-back-your-time/summary/); [MindMapAI](https://mindmapai.app/mind-mapping/buy-back-your-time-by-dan-martell) (secondary; not confirmed against the book)

**Buyback Rate (Martell)**
- Formula: annual income ÷ 2,000 hours = effective hourly rate; ÷ 4 = Buyback Rate, the 4 standing for a target 4× ROI on the money spent. Example: $100,000 ÷ 2,000 = $50/h → $12.50 BBR. Rule: don't do anything you could pay someone ≤ BBR to do. — [Entrepreneur interview with Martell](https://www.entrepreneur.com/living/how-much-should-pay-other-people-to-take-work-off-your/443766); [Next Big Idea Club excerpt](https://nextbigideaclub.com/magazine/buy-back-time-get-unstuck-reclaim-freedom-build-empire-bookbite/39964/)
- Sources differ on what counts as "income" (the book excerpt includes discretionary expenses; the interview says probably exclude non-core business expenses). — [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/living/how-much-should-pay-other-people-to-take-work-off-your/443766); [Next Big Idea Club](https://nextbigideaclub.com/magazine/buy-back-time-get-unstuck-reclaim-freedom-build-empire-bookbite/39964/)
- Martell's quote: no one "should be performing a work task that they could outsource for one-fourth... of their current effective hourly rate." — [Readingraphics](https://readingraphics.com/book-summary-buy-back-your-time/)

**Replacement Ladder and Camcorder Method (Martell)**
- Ladder rungs in order: Admin assistant → Delivery → Marketing → Sales → Leadership. Each rung involves a key hire, ownership and a "feeling" the founder gains. — [Readingraphics](https://readingraphics.com/book-summary-buy-back-your-time/) (secondary)
- Camcorder Method: record yourself doing the task, then have the new person write up the process from the recording. Summaries say this saves founder hours and confirms the new person understood. Part of the "4 Cs" playbook method: Camcorder, Course, Cadence, Checklist. — [Readingraphics](https://readingraphics.com/book-summary-buy-back-your-time/); [Martell Method podcast episode description](https://audioscrape.com/podcast/the-martell-method-w-dan-martell/episode/buy-back-your-time-in-35-minutes--book-summary) (secondary)
- The "Pain Line" is the point where the founder's own effort can no longer fuel growth and work starts to cost health and relationships. Typical responses are sabotage, stall or sell. "Don't hire to grow your business. Hire to buy back your time." — [Readingraphics](https://readingraphics.com/book-summary-buy-back-your-time/)

**Delegate & Elevate (EOS / Gino Wickman, *How to Be a Great Boss*)**
- Map your activities on two axes, love/like vs don't-like and great/good vs not-good, into four quadrants. Elevate into Love/Great ("Unique Ability"), keep Like/Good, and delegate the two bottom quadrants. EOS teaches that at least 80% of your activities should sit in the top two. List activities over "a week or longer" first. — [EOS Worldwide, Delegate and Elevate](https://www.eosworldwide.com/delegate-and-elevate); [EOS Great Boss Toolkit (PDF)](https://www.eosworldwide.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Great-Boss-Toolkit.pdf); [Business Builder newsletter](https://businessbuilder.beehiiv.com/p/delegate-elevate)

**SOPs (EOS Three-Step Process Documenter)**
- (1) Identify core processes: most companies have 5–12; give each an owner. (2) Document using 20/80: capture the 20% of steps that produce 80% of results; typically 1–2 pages, 7–15 major steps, each with 1–5 sub-points (who/what/where/when/how). (3) Package them somewhere easy to find. Then train, measure and reinforce until "Followed By All" (FBA). — [EOS Worldwide, Three-Step Process Documenter](https://www.eosworldwide.com/three-step-process-documenter); [Strety EOS Core Process guide](https://strety.com/blog/eos-core-process-complete-guide/)

### Inferences
- **Delegation-readiness module** (synthesis):
  - (a) **Audit mode**: a 2-week, 15-minute-interval prompt (or calendar-derived log) that tags each entry with domain, $–$$$$ value and energy (+/−). Output: a DRIP/Delegate-&-Elevate 2×2 and hours per quadrant.
  - (b) **Buyback Rate calculator**: income input ÷ 2,000 ÷ 4. Every recurring task with an estimated outsourcing cost ≤ BBR gets a "delegate" flag.
  - (c) **Per-task disposition**: Keep / Delegate / Automate / Eliminate. Tasks marked Delegate prompt "record next time you do this" (Camcorder) and create a draft SOP.
  - (d) **SOP library**: one page per process, 7–15 steps, owner, tools, checklist; "last performed" and "documented?" status; mapped to Replacement Ladder rungs (Admin, Delivery, Marketing, Sales, Leadership).
  - (e) **Readiness score per rung**: % of that rung's recurring tasks with an SOP. This tells the founder when a first VA or contractor could take a rung over.
- The two core processes for this persona are client delivery (onboarding → kickoff → milestones → handover) and lead-to-close (Q2 stages). Both can start as the founder's own checklists and later become team SOPs, which fits EOS's 5–12 core-process range.
- AI angle (inference): the app can draft an SOP from a screen recording or voice memo transcript, which is the Camcorder step done by software instead of a hire.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the primary text of *Buy Back Your Time*. DRIP quadrant names/definitions, Camcorder steps, the 4 Cs and the Replacement Ladder come from secondary summaries that broadly agree but were not checked against the book.
- Martell's Perfect Week and Preloaded Year concepts were mentioned in summaries without enough detail to report mechanics.

## 6. Which EOS / Scaling Up concepts are worth adopting now as a solo founder, and which should wait for the company layer?

### Takeaway
EOS is designed for 10–250-person companies with a leadership team, and full rollout takes about two years. A near-solo founder should take only the lightweight time-boxing tools now: a personal one-page vision, 3–7 quarterly Rocks with 30/60/90-day milestones, a 5–15-number weekly Scorecard, a 7-day To-Do list, an Issues list with IDS, a short weekly L10-style review, and documented core processes. The Accountability Chart, People Analyzer, full L10 meetings, daily huddles and cascading priorities should wait until there are people to hold accountable.

### Cited Findings

**EOS components and tools**
- Six key components: Vision, People, Data, Issues, Process, Traction. Tools include the V/TO (Vision built in "Two sessions. Eight questions."), People Analyzer, Scorecard ("a handful of objective measurables"), IDS, the 3-Step Process Documenter and the L10 agenda. Quarterly sessions "Every 90 days"; annual session "Two days a year." — [EOS Worldwide, What is EOS](https://www.eosworldwide.com/blog/what-is-eos)
- Level 10 meeting (90 min): Segue 5 · Scorecard 5 · Rock Review 5 · Customer/Employee Headlines 5 · To-Do List 5 · IDS 60 · Conclude 5. "Same day. Same time. Same agenda. Every week." Scorecard = "Five to fifteen weekly numbers", reported on/off-track with no discussion. Rocks are reported on/off-track, and off-track items go to the Issues List. IDS takes the top 3 issues in priority order and goes after root causes. Everyone rates the meeting 1–10; the goal is "8 or better." Department meetings run 30–60 min. — [EOS Worldwide, Level 10 Meeting](https://www.eosworldwide.com/blog/level-10-meeting)
- Rocks: narrow to about 3–7 company Rocks per 90 days, because the chance of finishing at least 80% "decrease[s] significantly" above 7. — [EOS Worldwide, Creating the Best Rocks](https://www.eosworldwide.com/blog/creating-the-best-rocks). An implementer recommends 3 milestones per Rock (due day 30, 60, 90) and weekly on/off-track reporting. — [EOS Implementer, SMART Rocks](https://implementer.eosworldwide.com/implementer-post/the-basics-of-setting-eos-smart-rocks/). Sources disagree on whether SMART's "R" means Realistic or Relevant (same sources).
- Fit: EOS is generally described as best for privately held companies with 10–250 employees and a multi-person leadership team (Visionary + Integrator). Below about 10 people it can feel like overhead; one source says it is less effective under 5. Full adoption takes at least about 2 years. — [Strety, Evaluating EOS for small to mid-size companies](https://strety.com/blog/evaluating-eos-for-small-to-mid-size-companies/); [SaaSRise on EOS in SaaS](https://www.saasrise.com/blog/how-to-implement-eos-in-saas-how-saas-founders-actually-make-the-entrepreneurial-operating-system-work-and-where-it-breaks) (implementer/consultant sources, not formal EOS rules)

**Scaling Up (Verne Harnish, Rockefeller Habits 2.0)**
- One-Page Strategic Plan: vision/mission, core values, critical numbers, quarterly priorities, key initiatives. The plan aims to align everyone on the "#1 thing" for the quarter. — [Align, Scaling Up toolset](https://aligntoday.com/?p=15057); [Rocketclicks review of Rockefeller Habits](https://rocketclicks.com/company-culture/wwr-rockefeller-habits/) (secondary)
- Meeting rhythm: daily huddle (5–15 min: news, top priorities/KPIs, where stuck); weekly meeting of 30–90 min (Red/Yellow/Green status of quarterly priorities plus 1–2 topics); monthly management meeting; quarterly and annual planning. Harnish: "more people in less meetings, not more meetings with less people." — [Align, Meeting Rhythm](https://aligntoday.com/the-meeting-rhythm-to-scale-up-efficiently/) (secondary)
- The Four Decisions are People, Strategy, Execution and Cash. A summary describes them as hiring the best leaders and aligning on core values; strategy; execution without drama and profitably; and enough cash to get through hard times. — [Autymate Scaling Up summary](https://www.autymate.com/book-summary/scaling-up) (secondary; the full framework was not confirmed)

### Inferences
- **Adopt now (personal layer)**:
  - A one-page personal/company vision (a lightweight V/TO or OPSP with 10-year target, 3-year picture, 1-year goals, and an anchor).
  - 3–5 quarterly Rocks with 30/60/90 milestones. A near-solo founder needs fewer than the 3–7 company range.
  - A personal weekly Scorecard of 5–10 numbers (e.g., outreach sent, conversations booked, proposals out, revenue closed, delivery hours, content pieces shipped, cash in bank).
  - A 7-day To-Do list with a completion-rate target.
  - An Issues list worked through by IDS in a weekly self-review.
  - Delegate & Elevate / DRIP.
  - Core-process docs for delivery and sales.
- **Adopt with the co-founder now**: a 30–60-minute weekly co-founder L10 (scorecard, Rocks, headlines, To-Dos, IDS) and a quarterly planning day. This matches the EOS department-meeting length.
- **Defer to the company layer (10+ people or a real leadership team)**: Accountability Chart, apart from naming seats for the two co-founders (e.g., Visionary vs Integrator, or Sales/Marketing vs Delivery/Ops), which is useful early; People Analyzer and core values for hiring; full 90-min L10; Scaling Up daily huddles and cascading priorities; formal annual 2-day sessions; Followed-By-All process audits.
- **Data-model note**: build Rocks, Scorecard metrics, To-Dos, Issues and Processes as first-class entities with an "owner" field from day one, even if the owner is always "me". The company layer then only adds more owners and seats.

### Gaps
- Found no official EOS guidance for solo founders or two-person teams.
- Did not retrieve the eight V/TO questions or the full OPSP layout from primary sources.
- Scaling Up details are from consultant blogs; the book's exact meeting agendas were not verified.

## 7. What does a founder's ideal weekly cadence look like?

### Takeaway
The sources combine into a consistent rhythm: plan the week at the start (or Sunday, per Dorsey), protect maker mornings, group calls into afternoon "office hours", run a daily sales/follow-up block, batch content once a week, and close with a fixed Friday review (Scorecard, pipeline, Waiting For/commitments, Rocks, Issues). Add quarterly Rock planning and pre-planned Free Days on top. No single authoritative "ideal week" exists; this is a synthesis.

### Cited Findings
- Weekly meetings run "Same day. Same time. Same agenda. Every week." with a fixed sequence: scorecard → Rocks → headlines → To-Dos → IDS. — [EOS Worldwide, Level 10](https://www.eosworldwide.com/blog/level-10-meeting)
- Weekly pipeline review (Kazanjy: 2 hours late Wednesday afternoon) plus a daily "floating hour" for pipeline follow-up. — [Holloway / Kazanjy](https://holloway.com/g/founding-sales/sections/pipeline-management)
- Put meetings at the end of the day and in office-hour blocks to protect maker time. — [Paul Graham](http://www.paulgraham.com/makersschedule.html)
- Dorsey: Mon = management/running the company; Wed = marketing/growth; Sun = reflection, feedback and strategy. — [CNN Money](https://money.cnn.com/2011/11/13/technology/dorsey_techonomy/)
- Sullivan: plan Free Days deliberately ("don't have an unplanned Free Day"); use Buffer Days around time off for preparation. — [Strategic Coach transcript](https://charm.strategiccoach.com/resources/podcasts/inside-strategic-coach/135794272)
- GTD weekly review: check Waiting For, chase stalled items, send reminders for overdue items, delete what no longer matters. — [Super Productivity, Weekly Review](https://super-productivity.com/blog/gtd-weekly-review-guide/); [Forte Labs](https://fortelabs.com/blog/the-weekly-review-is-an-operating-system/)
- Scaling Up rhythm stacks daily (5–15 min) → weekly (30–90 min) → monthly → quarterly → annual. — [Align, Meeting Rhythm](https://aligntoday.com/the-meeting-rhythm-to-scale-up-efficiently/)
- EOS quarterly sessions every 90 days; Rocks with 30/60/90-day milestones. — [EOS Worldwide, What is EOS](https://www.eosworldwide.com/blog/what-is-eos); [EOS Implementer](https://implementer.eosworldwide.com/implementer-post/the-basics-of-setting-eos-smart-rocks/)
- Schedule focused work on your top three priorities in your sharpest hours and batch reactive work. — [echai.ventures listing of First Round/Trenchard](https://echai.ventures/startingup/productivity-time/how-do-i-track-where-my-hours-actually-go-so-i-know-what-to-cut-without-turning-tracking-into-another-chore) (attribution unverified)

### Inferences
- **Proposed default week for a near-solo co-founder** (synthesis, a template for the app's "Perfect Week" builder):
  - **Mon**: AM 30–45 min weekly planning (review Rocks, set the week's top 3, schedule commitments due, set the outreach quota), then a delivery maker block. PM client calls (office hours).
  - **Tue**: AM delivery maker block. PM sales block: outreach and discovery calls.
  - **Wed**: AM content hub writing (2–3 h). PM sales calls, then the weekly pipeline review (60–90 min, as Kazanjy does).
  - **Thu**: AM delivery maker block. PM client calls, then spoke batching (60–90 min).
  - **Fri**: AM delivery or company-building (SOPs, Camcorder recordings, finance) as a Buffer-style half day. PM weekly review (60–90 min) and, if applicable, a 30–60 min co-founder L10.
  - **Daily**: 15-min morning plan; 60–90 min outreach and follow-up block driven by due next-steps; 15–30 min content engagement; 10-min end-of-day shutdown (capture new commitments and waiting-fors).
  - **Weekend**: at least one protected Free Day; an optional 30-min Sunday reflection (Dorsey).
- **Friday review checklist**:
  1. Fill in the Scorecard numbers, mark each on/off-track.
  2. Pipeline: every deal has a dated next step; flag deals idle 7+ days or 14+ days in one stage.
  3. Commitments: I-owe overdue first, then Waiting-for to chase.
  4. Rocks: on/off-track.
  5. Issues: IDS the top 3.
  6. Personal CRM: who is due to reconnect.
  7. Decision journal: entries due for outcome review.
  8. Audit snapshot: hours by domain vs target, and tasks to delegate/automate/eliminate.
  9. Next week: maker blocks placed first, Free Day protected.
- **Monthly**: 2-week time-and-energy audit every quarter or twice a year; review Buyback Rate and SOP coverage. **Quarterly**: set the next 3–5 Rocks with 30/60/90 milestones and refresh the one-page vision. **Annually**: plan Free Days and vacations first (Sullivan), then quarterly themes.

### Gaps
- No source presents a validated "ideal week" for a services-plus-sales co-founder; the cadence above is a synthesis of the individual frameworks.
- Found no evidence on which review day (Friday vs Sunday vs Monday) works better; practitioners differ (Dorsey uses Sunday, Kazanjy's pipeline review is Wednesday, EOS just says "same day every week").
