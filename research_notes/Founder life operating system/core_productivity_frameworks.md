# Core Personal Productivity and Life-Organization Frameworks, and How They Combine into One Goal-to-Action Hierarchy

Scope note: these notes are for the design of an all-in-one personal life OS app for a solo startup co-founder. Primary sources were used where they could be reached (gettingthingsdone.com, fortelabs.com, bulletjournal.com, fullfocus.co, calnewport.com, and the full text of Locke & Latham 2002). Several primary books (GTD, The 12 Week Year, Full Focus Planner, Eat That Frog, Measure What Matters) could not be read directly. For those, the notes rely on book-note excerpts and secondary summaries, and say so. Research date: October 2026.

---

## Q1. For each framework: what are its core entities, how do they relate, and which cadences does it prescribe?

### Takeaway
Every framework is some mix of three parts: an outcome layer (vision, goals, OKRs, 12-week goals), a commitment/inventory layer (projects, areas, next actions, logs, backlog) and a daily selection rule (contexts, Big 3, Ivy Lee 6, 1-3-5, the frog, time blocks, WIP limits). They differ mainly in which layer they treat as primary. GTD and BuJo start from the inventory and work up. Full Focus, 12WY and OKRs start from goals and work down. Time blocking, Ivy Lee, 1-3-5 and Kanban are pure execution-layer rules. The Wheel of Life and PARA Areas give the persistent "life areas" spine that the rest hang from.

### Cited Findings

#### GTD (Getting Things Done, David Allen)
- **Five steps (official wording):** Capture ("Collect what has your attention"), Clarify ("Process what it means"; decide if it is actionable; if yes, define the next action and, when more than one action is needed, the project; if no, trash it, file it as reference or defer it), Organize ("Put it where it belongs"), Reflect ("Review frequently"), Engage ("Simply do"). — [GTD: What is GTD](https://gettingthingsdone.com/what-is-gtd/)
- **Horizons of Focus (official "altitude map"), with definitions and the review frequency David Allen Co. suggests for each:**
  - *Horizon 5, Purpose & Principles:* "Ultimate intention for something, and the standards for its success." Frequency: "Whenever additional clarity, direction, alignment, and motivation are needed."
  - *Horizon 4, Vision:* "What it will look, sound, feel like with successful implementation. Long-term outcomes and ideal scenarios." Formats include life planning, "annual revisiting," "personal treasure maps." Frequency: whenever clarity is needed.
  - *Horizon 3, Goals & Objectives:* "What do we want and need to accomplish, specifically, within the next 12 to 24 months." Frequency: "Annually; quarterly reviews and recalibrations."
  - *Horizon 2, Areas of Focus & Accountability:* "Important spheres of work and life to be maintained at standards to 'keep the engines running.'" Formats: job descriptions, "personal lifestyle checklists," family responsibility designations. Frequency: "monthly personal check-in's; whenever job or life changes require reassessment."
  - *Horizon 1, Projects:* "Outcomes we want to achieve that require more than one action and which can be completed within a year." Formats: an overview list of all projects plus project plans. Frequency: "Weekly Review; whenever next action contents are not current."
  - *Ground, Calendar/Actions:* "Next physical, visible actions to take on any project or other outcome." Formats: "Calendar, Next Action lists (e.g. calls, errands, at home, at office, talk to boss about...); action folders." Frequency: "Multiple times daily."
  - "Priorities are determined from the top down." — [GTD Horizons of Focus PDF (2014/2015)](https://gettingthingsdone.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/2016-Levels-of-Your-Work.pdf); [GTD Horizons of Focus for determining priorities](https://gettingthingsdone.com/2018/12/the-gtd-horizons-of-focus-for-determining-your-priorities/)
- **Choosing what to do in the moment, four criteria in order:** context (the place, tool or person needed), time available, energy/resources, and priority. Priority is judged against the higher horizons. **Threefold model of daily work:** doing pre-defined work (from the calendar and next-action lists), doing work as it shows up, and defining work (processing inputs). — [GTD: Choosing what to do (2023)](https://gettingthingsdone.com/2023/01/choosing-what-to-do/)
- **Two-minute rule:** if a next action takes under two minutes, do it now instead of tracking it. Per forum quotes of the 2015 edition, the cutoff sits where storing and tracking an item would take longer than doing it. Practitioners still argue over whether the rule applies only while processing or also at the next-action level. — [GTD podcast Ep. 79](https://gettingthingsdone.com/2020/05/the-two-minute-rule-2/); [GTD forum thread](https://forum.gettingthingsdone.com/threads/i-got-the-2-minute-rule-wrong.17724/page-2)
- **Waiting For and Someday/Maybe lists:** practitioners treat "waiting for X" as a valid next action that lives on the Waiting For list. Someday/Maybe holds projects you mean to pursue later but are not committing to now. — [GTD forum (two-minute rule / list threads)](https://forum.gettingthingsdone.com/threads/i-got-the-2-minute-rule-wrong.17724/page-2)
- **Weekly Review:** commonly taught in three phases. *Get Clear* (collect loose papers, empty inboxes, empty your head), *Get Current* (review next actions, past and upcoming calendar, Waiting For and the projects list), *Get Creative* (review Someday/Maybe, activate or delete items, generate new ideas). These are secondary descriptions of the official checklist. — [Doist: The Weekly Review](https://doist.com/blog/weekly-review); [dsebastien: Weekly Review](https://concepts.dsebastien.net/concept/weekly-review/)
- **Good at / bad at:** strong at getting everything out of your head and at handling a high volume of inputs. Allen himself says full adoption is rare and that "falling off the GTD wagon" is "almost universal" (see Q4). Its upper horizons are thinly specified in the method compared with its ground-level mechanics. — [David Allen Substack, June 9 2025](https://davidallen.substack.com/p/partial-or-complete-gtd)

#### PARA (Tiago Forte)
- **Entities:** *Projects* are "short-term efforts (in your work or personal life) that you take on with a certain goal in mind." *Areas* are "important parts of your work and life that require ongoing attention" and have no end date. *Resources* are "resources on a range of topics you're interested in and learning about." *Archives* hold "anything from the previous three categories that is no longer active." — [Forte Labs: The PARA Method](https://fortelabs.com/blog/para/)
- **Rule:** organize "according to the projects and goals you are committed to right now," meaning by actionability rather than by subject. Each of the four top-level folders holds only "a small number of subfolders." Items move to Archives when projects finish or pause, when areas go inactive, or when interest in a resource fades. — [Forte Labs: PARA](https://fortelabs.com/blog/para/)
- **Book:** *The PARA Method* was published August 15, 2023 (Profile Books edition). Reader reviews are mixed, and one calls it padded relative to Forte's free material. — [Profile Books](https://profilebooks.com/work/the-para-method/); [Productive Happiness review](https://productivehappiness.substack.com/p/the-para-method-is-here-a-short-review)
- **Good at / bad at:** strong as a filing and knowledge layer that shares Project/Area semantics with GTD Horizons 1 and 2. On its own it has no task-selection rule and no goal layer, and the core article does not specify a review cadence.

#### Building a Second Brain (CODE)
- **Capture** only what "resonates," using read-later apps, highlight exports and web clippers, all routed into one notes app. **Organize** with PARA. To start, move all existing files into a dated Archive instead of sorting them, and park new items in an Inbox until filed. **Distill** with Progressive Summarization (layered highlighting and summarizing, adding value each time a note is touched). **Express** through "Intermediate Packets" (meeting notes, outlines, research lists): finish one per work session, save it back, reuse it, and ship "v1.0" early. — [Forte Labs: BASB overview (updated Nov 23, 2023)](https://fortelabs.com/blog/basboverview/)
- **Cadence:** process the Inbox weekly in batches (roughly 10 to 20 items in a few minutes). Finish one Intermediate Packet per session. Distill opportunistically. — [Forte Labs: BASB overview](https://fortelabs.com/blog/basboverview/)

#### Bullet Journal (Ryder Carroll)
- **Rapid logging symbols (official):** Task "•", Event "O", Note "–". Task states: Complete "X", Migrated forward ">" (to a later Monthly Log or a Collection), Scheduled backward "<" (to the Future Log), Irrelevant (struck through). Signifiers add meaning, such as * for priority and ! for inspiration (secondary source). — [Bullet Journal: How to Bullet Journal](https://bulletjournal.com/pages/how-to-bullet-journal); [weekplan.net glossary](https://weekplan.net/academy/glossary/bullet-journaling)
- **Modules:** *Index* (the first pages, topic plus page number). *Future Log* (a year overview split by month; when a month arrives, its entries move into that Monthly Log). *Monthly Log* (calendar page on the left, task page on the right). *Daily Log* (dated, rapid-logged as the day goes). *Collections* (topic lists). — [Bullet Journal: How to](https://bulletjournal.com/pages/how-to-bullet-journal); [BuJo FAQ: Future Log](https://bulletjournal.com/blogs/faq/how-to-make-a-future-log)
- **Migration:** at each month's end, review open tasks, mark finished ones X, strike out irrelevant ones, and rewrite still-relevant ones into the new Monthly Log or a Collection (marked ">"). "If an entry isn't worth the effort to rewrite, then it's probably not that important." The community also practices yearly migration into a new notebook. — [Bullet Journal: Migration 101](https://bulletjournal.com/blogs/faq/migration); [BuJo: Yearly Migration](https://bulletjournal.com/blogs/bulletjournalist/yearly-migration/)
- **Recent:** bulletjournal.com now sells "Foundation" and "Advanced" plans plus a community (© 2026 footer). The Bullet Journal Companion iOS app is at v2.5 (daily prompts, widgets, tutorials; $4.99), release date unconfirmed. — [Bullet Journal: How to](https://bulletjournal.com/pages/how-to-bullet-journal); [App Store: Bullet Journal Companion](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1201419241)

#### Eisenhower Matrix
- The source is Eisenhower's 1954 address at Northwestern University, where he quoted an unnamed "former college president": "I have two kinds of problems, the urgent and the important…" The coiner of the adage is unknown. Stephen Covey later popularized the 2x2 (urgent/important) in *The 7 Habits*. — [Quote Investigator](https://quoteinvestigator.com/2014/05/09/urgent/); [ClearanceJobs](https://news.clearancejobs.com/2021/10/26/the-great-decider-setting-priorities-with-the-eisenhower-box/)
- The usual mechanics are Q1 do, Q2 schedule, Q3 delegate, Q4 delete. — [Doist: Eisenhower Matrix](https://doist.com/blog/eisenhower-matrix/)

#### Time blocking / timeboxing (Cal Newport)
- **Mechanics:** a paper sheet with two columns. The left column has two lines per hour, split into blocks labelled with specific assignments. The right column holds notes. He spends "ten to twenty minutes every evening" building the next day's blocks from task lists, the calendar, and his weekly and quarterly planning notes. When the day goes off-plan he leaves room to re-draw blocks ("make corrections as needed"). Reactive work gets its own scheduled blocks. Newport claims a time-blocked 40-hour week "produces the same amount of output as a 60+ hour work week pursued without structure." That is his own estimate, with no data offered. — [Cal Newport: Deep Habits, planning every minute](https://calnewport.com/deep-habits-the-importance-of-planning-every-minute-of-your-work-day/)
- **Multi-scale planning:** quarterly plan, then weekly plan, then daily time-block plan, with each feeding the next, plus a fixed work schedule, a shutdown ritual and a separate timeless values document. This comes from secondary summaries of Newport's podcast. — [Video highlight: Deep Questions, multi-scale planning](https://videohighlight.com/v/ZHjK2XxcjQk); [Huberman AI summary](https://ai.hubermanlab.com/s/SBJgXQPb)
- **2024 update, *Slow Productivity*:** three principles: do fewer things, work at a natural pace, obsess over quality. It critiques "pseudo-productivity" (treating visible activity as a proxy for useful work) and recommends formal workload management, such as quotas and separating "active" from "waiting" work. — [Cal Newport: My new book, Slow Productivity](https://calnewport.com/my-new-book-slow-productivity/); [Cal Newport: The Workload Fairy Tale](https://calnewport.com/the-workload-fairy-tale/)

#### The Ideal Week and the Full Focus System (Michael Hyatt / Full Focus)
- **Annual goals:** "Define 8 Annual Goals, and get specific," using the SMARTER framework. For each goal, record key motivations ranked by importance, the first few actions, and how you will celebrate. — [Full Focus: The System](https://fullfocus.co/system/)
- **Quarterly goals:** assign three or fewer Annual Goals to each quarter (the "Quarterly Big 3"). Exceeding three "risks burnout." — [Full Focus: The System](https://fullfocus.co/system/)
- **Weekly Big 3:** the quarterly goals broken into weekly objectives that usually take several days each, set during the Weekly Preview. — [Full Focus: The System](https://fullfocus.co/system/); [Full Focus: 3 simple tools to win back your week](https://fullfocus.co/3-simple-tools-to-win-back-your-week/)
- **Weekly Preview, five steps:** (1) review personal and professional wins, (2) assess what worked and what didn't (keep / improve / start / stop), (3) reassign unfinished tasks by delegating or rescheduling, (4) note big deadlines, events and rejuvenation, and protect non-work hours, (5) set the Weekly Big 3. — [Full Focus: The System](https://fullfocus.co/system/)
- **Daily Big 3:** three priorities each day, chosen by what is most important, what is urgent, and what moves the Weekly Big 3. Tasks, schedule and notes live in one place. — [Full Focus: The System](https://fullfocus.co/system/); [Hyatt on Goodreads blog: The Key to a More Productive Day](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/21505319-the-key-to-a-more-productive-day)
- **Ideal Week:** a template for blocking work time, setting themes for different days of the week and embedding rituals. Practitioners compare the actual calendar against it to find gaps, and warn that "open" gaps are often underestimated tasks rather than free time. — [Full Focus: Your Ideal Week](https://fullfocus.co/idealweek/); [Full Focus: 3 keys to crafting your Ideal Week](https://fullfocus.co/3-keys-to-crafting-your-ideal-week-to-win-back-your-time/)
- The company now positions the Full Focus System as the base for "corporate training and strategic planning" as well as planners. — [Full Focus: Team](https://fullfocus.co/team/)

#### The 12 Week Year (Brian Moran and Michael Lennington)
- **Entities:** a long-term vision, then 12-week goals (1 to 3 per cycle), then tactics (specific, measurable weekly actions per goal), then a weekly plan, then a weekly score. — [LogRocket: 12 Week Year approach](https://blog.logrocket.com/product-management/12-week-year-approach); [Goodreads book notes p.5](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/18961505-the-12-week-year/153805008-javy?page=5)
- **Lead vs. lag:** lag measures are end results (weight, revenue). Lead measures are the actions that drive them (workouts, sales calls). — [Goodreads book notes p.2](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/18961505-the-12-week-year/153805008-javy?page=2)
- **Execution score:** tactics completed ÷ tactics planned × 100, scored weekly. It measures execution, not results. Book text: "if you successfully complete 85 percent of the activities in your weekly plan, then you will most likely achieve your objectives." — [Goodreads book notes](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/18961505-the-12-week-year/153805008-javy?page=2); [Yu Wenhao: 12 Week Year guide](https://yu-wenhao.com/en/blog/12-week-year-guide/)
- **Weekly routine:** score your week, plan your week, attend a WAM (Weekly Accountability Meeting). The WAM agenda: each member reports results to date, their weekly execution score and intentions for next week, then the group discusses what is working and offers encouragement. It runs about 15 to 20 minutes with a partner or small group. Time is protected through "strategic" and "buffer" blocks treated as non-negotiable appointments. — [Goodreads book notes p.5](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/18961505-the-12-week-year/153805008-javy?page=5); [dsebastien: 12 Week Year](https://concepts.dsebastien.net/concept/12-week-year/); [Goodreads notes (Liisa Aavik)](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/18961505-the-12-week-year/27181376-liisa-aavik?page=1)

#### Personal OKRs (Andy Grove / John Doerr, *Measure What Matters*)
- **Limits:** 3 to 5 objectives at a time with 3 to 5 key results each. One glossary says 2 to 5 KRs. — [Shortform: Measure What Matters summary](https://shortform.com/summary/measure-what-matters-summary-john-doerr); [Hyring OKR glossary](https://hyring.com/free-hr-toolkit/hr-glossary/objectives-and-key-results-okr)
- **Grading:** scored 0 to 1.0. The Google-era "sweet spot" is 0.6 to 0.7, and consistent 1.0s mean the goals were not ambitious enough. Sources disagree here: one Measure What Matters excerpt sets the expected value at 0.8. — [OKRs primer (Speakerdeck)](https://speakerdeck.com/escapist/okrs-a-primer); [Hyring](https://hyring.com/free-hr-toolkit/hr-glossary/objectives-and-key-results-okr); [Measure What Matters excerpts](https://polarr.notion.site/Excerpts-and-quotes-from-Measure-What-Matters-Required-Reading-3db4442f64604d54865aafb630df437e)
- **Personal use:** "without key results to make them actionable, objectives are just wishes." Example: a marathon under four hours with supporting KRs. Individuals set personal OKRs to align with team and company OKRs. — [Todoist: OKRs guide](https://www.todoist.com/zh-TW/productivity-methods/okrs-objectives-key-results); [OKRs primer](https://speakerdeck.com/escapist/okrs-a-primer)

#### Ivy Lee method
- At the end of each workday, write the six most important things to do tomorrow, rank them, work on #1 until it is finished, move down the list in order, and carry unfinished items into the next day's list of six. The origin story (Ivy Lee, Charles Schwab, Bethlehem Steel, c. 1918, a $25,000 cheque) varies in date and detail across sources. — [James Clear: Ivy Lee](https://jamesclear.com/ivy-lee); [Fabric: Ivy Lee method](https://fabric.so/learn/ivy-lee-method)

#### Eat the Frog (Brian Tracy, *Eat That Frog!*, 2001)
- Do the most important, most-resisted task first each morning. "If you must eat two frogs, eat the ugliest one first." Plan the next day the night before, and apply 80/20. The Mark Twain attribution is unverified. The most common failure is "picking the wrong frog." — [Graham Mann: Eat That Frog notes](https://grahammann.net/book-notes/eat-that-frog-brian-tracy); [Griply: Eat the Frog explained](https://griply.app/guides/productivity/eat-the-frog-meaning-the-brian-tracy-method-explained)

#### 1-3-5 rule (Alex Cavoulacos, The Muse)
- Each day: 1 big task, 3 medium tasks, 5 small tasks (small usually 30 minutes or less), selected from a master list. Described in *The New Rules of Work*. — [Todoist: 1-3-5 method](https://www.todoist.com/productivity-methods/1-3-5-method); [The Everygirl](https://theeverygirl.com/1-3-5-rule-for-productivity/); [Early: 1-3-5 rule](https://early.app/blog/1-3-5-rule/)

#### Personal Kanban (Jim Benson and Tonianne DeMaria Barry)
- **Two rules:** "visualize your work and limit your work in process." The simplest board is Backlog, Doing, Done. A WIP limit of about 3 (2 to 3 cards in progress) is the common starting point, and new cards are pulled only after finishing current ones. — [Tech Lead Journal #97, Jim Benson](https://techleadjournal.dev/episodes/97/); [iSixSigma: Personal Kanban](https://www.isixsigma.com/kanban-tool/personal-kanban/); [TasksBoard](https://tasksboard.com/blog/personal-kanban-board)

#### Wheel of Life / life areas
- Credited to Paul J. Meyer (Success Motivation Institute), 1960s. "Wheel of Life" is a trademark of Meyer Resource Group. There is no fixed list of areas; about 8 is typical, for example Health & Fitness, Career/Work, Finances, Relationships & Family, Personal Growth, Fun & Recreation, Physical Environment, Spirituality/Purpose. Each area is rated (usually 1 to 10) to show imbalance. — [MindTools: Wheel of Life](https://www.mindtools.com/ak6jd6w/the-wheel-of-life/); [Griply: Wheel of Life](https://griply.app/guides/life-planning/the-wheel-of-life-what-it-is-and-how-to-use-it); [Noomii Wheel of Life](https://wheeloflife.noomii.com/); [PositivePsychology.com](https://positivepsychology.com/wheel-of-life-coaching/)

#### Recent updates (2024–2026) found
- Cal Newport, *Slow Productivity* (2024): "do fewer things" and workload caps. — [calnewport.com](https://calnewport.com/my-new-book-slow-productivity/)
- David Allen, "Partial, or Complete GTD?" (June 9, 2025): full implementation is rare and "phenomenal[ly]" different from partial use; falling off is almost universal. — [David Allen Substack](https://davidallen.substack.com/p/partial-or-complete-gtd)
- GTD podcast Episode #339, "The Higher Horizons of Focus" (December 2025). A search summary describes it as framing Vision as a 3 to 5 year picture. — [GTD Ep. 339](https://gettingthingsdone.com/2025/12/the-higher-horizons-of-focus/)
- Bullet Journal membership plans and Companion app v2.5 (dates unconfirmed). — [bulletjournal.com](https://bulletjournal.com/pages/how-to-bullet-journal); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1201419241)
- Thomas Frank's Ultimate Brain 3.0 for Notion (PARA plus GTD-style processing plus goals; date unconfirmed). — [Thomas Frank: Ultimate Brain](https://thomasjfrank.com/brain)

### Inferences
- **Canonical object set the app needs** (union of the frameworks, deduplicated): Purpose/Values statement; Vision (3 to 5 years); Life Area (Wheel of Life = GTD H2 = PARA Area); Annual Goal (H3, up to about 8 per Full Focus); Quarterly/12-week Goal or Objective with Key Results or lag measures (≤3 active); Project (multi-step outcome completable within about a year; links to 0..1 goal and exactly 1 area); Task/Next Action (fields: context, time estimate, energy, due/scheduled date, waiting-for person, 12WY "tactic" flag with weekly target); Habit/Routine (recurring lead measure attached to an Area or Goal); Calendar Event/Time Block; Inbox Item; Note/Resource (PARA R and A); Log entry (BuJo-style rapid log: task/event/note); Reviews (daily, weekly, monthly/area, quarterly, annual) as first-class records holding scores and reflections.
- **Cadence consensus:** daily (pick 3 to 6 items or time-block them; plan the evening before per Newport, Ivy Lee and Tracy), weekly (GTD Weekly Review ≈ Full Focus Weekly Preview ≈ 12WY score + plan + WAM), monthly (BuJo migration ≈ GTD H2 area check-in), quarterly/12-weekly (Full Focus Quarterly Big 3, 12WY cycle, OKR period, GTD H3 recalibration, Newport quarterly plan), annual (goals and vision). Sources that disagree on mechanics still agree on these cadences.
- **Strengths and weaknesses in one line each:** GTD covers inputs and inventory well but its upper horizons are weak. PARA files well but has no prioritization. BuJo builds reflection through rewriting friction but does not scale digitally (rewriting is the feature). Eisenhower is a sorting heuristic, not a system. Time blocking gives realism about capacity but is brittle without re-planning. Full Focus gives the clearest numeric cascade (8 → 3 → Weekly 3 → Daily 3). 12WY has the best execution feedback loop (lead measures + score + WAM) but says little about non-goal maintenance work. OKRs work well for stretch outcomes and team alignment but are too heavy for small personal tasks. Ivy Lee, 1-3-5 and the frog are daily selection rules only. Kanban gives WIP discipline and visual flow but no goal layer. The Wheel of Life gives balance diagnosis but no execution.

### Gaps
- Could not access the full GTD book text for the official Weekly Review checklist wording. The Get Clear/Current/Creative structure comes from secondary sources.
- The Full Focus page states 8 annual goals. Earlier Hyatt material (*Your Best Year Ever*) is commonly cited as recommending 7 to 10 goals split between "achievement" and "habit" goals, but I could not verify that split on fullfocus.co in this session. Official Ideal Week mechanics (e.g., "front stage / back stage / off stage" day themes) and Full Focus daily rituals (startup/shutdown) were not confirmed from a primary page.
- 12WY time-block types (strategic block about 3 hours, buffer blocks, breakout block) and the "13th week" are commonly described in the book but were not confirmed from an accessible source. The primary site 12weekyear.com was not fetched.
- Newport's minimum block length and his "overflow/conditional block" conventions are not in the fetched post. They are described in his Time-Block Planner materials, which I did not access.
- No 2024–2026 changes were found for Full Focus, 12WY, PARA/BASB (last update Nov 2023), Eisenhower, Ivy Lee, 1-3-5 or Personal Kanban.

## Q2. Which frameworks complement each other, which conflict, and what do experienced practitioners actually combine?

### Takeaway
The frameworks combine well when each is assigned to one layer: Wheel of Life/Areas for the spine, Full Focus/12WY/OKRs for goals, GTD for capture and inventory, PARA/BASB for reference, time blocking/Big 3/Kanban WIP for daily execution, and a GTD-style weekly review plus a 12WY score for the feedback loop. Conflicts appear when two frameworks try to own the same layer: GTD's in-the-moment choosing vs. pre-committed Big 3 and time blocks, BuJo's manual migration vs. digital auto-rollover, and different goal counts (Full Focus 8 a year, 12WY 1 to 3 per cycle, OKR 3 to 5).

### Cited Findings
- **GTD + 12 Week Year (practitioner):** a GTD forum user runs both because "they complement each other perfectly". GTD captures and organizes everything, and 12WY adds focus on execution. They map 12WY tactics to GTD next actions and cap themselves at "a maximum of 14 tactics per week in total." Another user calls 12WY "a GTD supplement." — [GTD forum post](https://forum.gettingthingsdone.com/posts/145854); [GTD forum](https://forum.gettingthingsdone.com/goto/post?id=145848)
- **GTD + PARA (practitioner pattern):** the common split is GTD for daily tasks (e.g., in Todoist) and PARA for file and note structure (e.g., in Notion). This comes from a low-credibility aggregator and is echoed in practitioner forums. — [JobCannon: GTD vs PARA vs PKM](https://jobcannon.io/tw/skills/personal-productivity-systems); [Everdo forum: GTD and what more?](https://forum.everdo.net/t/gtd-and-what-more/1341)
- **Commercial all-in-one combination (Thomas Frank, Ultimate Brain for Notion):** tasks, projects, notes and goals in one workspace, organized with "the P.A.R.A. organization method" and offering "advanced GTD®-style processing." Quick capture sends tasks and notes into separate inboxes that hold items until they are assigned to a project, area or resource. It includes Today / Next 7 Days views and board views. Price was about $129 in 2023. — [Thomas Frank: Ultimate Brain](https://thomasjfrank.com/brain); [Recall summary of Thomas Frank video](https://www.recall.it/summary/thomas-frank-explains/the-ultimate-second-brain-setup-in-notion); [Gumroad](https://thomasfrank.gumroad.com/l/ultimatebrain)
- **BASB explicitly uses PARA** as its Organize step, so these two are designed to fit together. — [Forte Labs: BASB](https://fortelabs.com/blog/basboverview/)
- **GTD's own model already holds the goal layers**: Horizons 3 to 5 sit above Projects, with annual and quarterly goal reviews. So adding Full Focus/12WY/OKRs fills in GTD's thinly specified upper horizons rather than contradicting them. — [GTD Horizons PDF](https://gettingthingsdone.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/2016-Levels-of-Your-Work.pdf)
- **Selection-rule tension:** GTD picks the next action in the moment by context, then time, then energy, then priority. — [GTD: Choosing what to do](https://gettingthingsdone.com/2023/01/choosing-what-to-do/). Full Focus pre-commits a Daily Big 3. — [Full Focus: System](https://fullfocus.co/system/). Newport pre-assigns every work hour to blocks the evening before. — [Cal Newport](https://calnewport.com/deep-habits-the-importance-of-planning-every-minute-of-your-work-day/). GTD's own "threefold model" leaves room for both, as "pre-defined work" alongside work as it shows up. — [GTD: Choosing what to do](https://gettingthingsdone.com/2023/01/choosing-what-to-do/)
- **Measurement compatibility:** OKRs grade outcomes and treat 0.6 to 0.7 as success. — [OKRs primer](https://speakerdeck.com/escapist/okrs-a-primer). 12WY scores execution of lead actions and treats 85% as the bar. — [Goodreads 12WY notes](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/18961505-the-12-week-year/153805008-javy?page=2). These measure different things (lag vs. lead), so they can coexist on one goal.
- **BuJo migration depends on manual rewriting as a filter** ("if an entry isn't worth the effort to rewrite… it's probably not that important"). — [BuJo Migration 101](https://bulletjournal.com/blogs/faq/migration)
- **Scaling to a team:** goal-setting research finds that when individuals' specific, difficult goals align with the group's goal, group performance improves. "Without such alignment, personal goals have a detrimental effect on a group's performance" (Seijts & Latham 2000b). — [Locke & Latham 2002](https://goal-lab.psych.umn.edu/orgpsych/2020/readings/5.%20Motivation/Locke%20&%20Latham%20(2002).pdf). GTD's own Horizons document uses an enterprise example (Gracie's Gardens) and formats such as "off-sites with partners, board, team." — [GTD Horizons PDF](https://gettingthingsdone.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/2016-Levels-of-Your-Work.pdf). Individuals set personal OKRs to align with team and company OKRs. — [OKRs primer](https://speakerdeck.com/escapist/okrs-a-primer). The 12WY WAM is a group ritual by design. — [Goodreads 12WY notes p.5](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/18961505-the-12-week-year/153805008-javy?page=5). Newport (via Farkas) argues that individual systems like GTD fail knowledge workers because the problem sits at the organizational level. — [Meredith Farkas: The productivity trap](https://meredithfarkas.substack.com/p/the-productivity-trap)

### Inferences
- **Recommended layer assignment ("one owner per layer"):**
  - *Spine:* Life Areas (Wheel of Life categories = GTD H2 = PARA Areas). One shared list with the same IDs everywhere.
  - *Direction:* Purpose/Vision (GTD H4/H5) leads to Annual Goals (Full Focus) and then Quarterly/12-week Goals. Express quarterly goals as OKR-style objectives with KRs (lag) plus 12WY tactics (lead). Use one goal object with optional KR and lead-measure fields rather than three separate goal systems.
  - *Inventory:* GTD Projects + Next Actions + Waiting For + Someday/Maybe. PARA Projects equal GTD Projects (same object, with notes and files attached).
  - *Reference:* PARA Resources/Archives with BASB distillation, attached to Projects and Areas.
  - *Execution:* Weekly Big 3 (drawn from active goals' tactics and projects), then Daily Big 3 (or Ivy Lee 6 / 1-3-5 as user-selectable "daily shapes"), placed into time blocks on the calendar. Kanban board views with a WIP limit apply to projects (e.g., ≤3 to 5 "Doing" projects).
  - *Feedback:* the weekly review merges the GTD Weekly Review (clear/current/creative), the Full Focus Weekly Preview (wins, keep/improve/start/stop, set Big 3) and 12WY scoring (execution %). Monthly: area check-in plus BuJo-style migration of stale tasks. Quarterly: score KRs, run the "13th-week" style reflection, set the next ≤3 goals.
- **Where conflicts need a product decision:**
  - GTD context lists vs. time blocks: resolve by time-blocking the Big 3 and goal tactics, and leaving "GTD-context" blocks for everything else (e.g., an "@calls/@errands" block). Newport already schedules reactive blocks.
  - BuJo manual rewriting vs. digital auto-rollover: auto-rollover destroys BuJo's filter. A "migration" prompt that forces an explicit keep/schedule/drop decision on tasks older than N days keeps the benefit.
  - Goal counts: Full Focus allows 8 per year but ≤3 per quarter, 12WY uses 1 to 3 per cycle and OKR uses 3 to 5. Converge on "≤3 active per quarter" as the enforced default, with up to about 8 annual goals as a backlog.
  - Eisenhower vs. everything else: use it as a triage lens during the weekly review or inbox processing (urgent/important tags), not as a separate system.
- **For a solo founder, the combination with the most support from both practitioners and the sources** is GTD capture/inventory + PARA reference + Full Focus/12WY goal cascade with weekly scoring + time-blocked Daily Big 3. This is close to what Ultimate Brain and the GTD+12WY forum user each assemble.
- **Which frameworks scale to a small company later:** OKRs (built for orgs), 12WY (the WAM is a team or partner meeting by design), Kanban (team boards with WIP), GTD Horizons (the official document uses business examples), Full Focus (now sold as corporate training) and PARA (as a shared drive structure). BuJo, Ivy Lee, 1-3-5, the frog and the Wheel of Life stay personal. Design implication: make Goals/KRs, Projects and Areas shareable objects with an owner, and keep daily lists and logs private.

### Gaps
- No credible survey data was found on which combinations practitioners actually use or how often (e.g., r/gtd or r/productivity polls). The evidence is individual forum posts and commercial templates.
- Ali Abdaal's specific system (e.g., his *Feel-Good Productivity* framing or any "Life OS" templates) was not researched in this session.
- I did not find Forte Labs' or David Allen Co.'s own official statement on combining PARA with GTD.

## Q3. What is a sensible unified hierarchy from vision → life areas → annual/quarterly goals → projects → tasks → daily plan, and where do routines and habits plug in?

### Takeaway
GTD's Horizons of Focus already give the skeleton, with built-in review frequencies: purpose/vision (as needed or annual), goals (annual with quarterly recalibration), areas (monthly), projects (weekly), actions (daily). Full Focus and the 12 Week Year add the numeric cascade (about 8 annual, then ≤3 quarterly, then Weekly Big 3, then Daily Big 3) and the lead-measure scoring that goal-setting research supports (proximal sub-goals plus feedback). Habits and routines belong as recurring lead measures attached to either an Area (maintenance standard) or a Goal (12WY tactic), not as a separate silo.

### Cited Findings
- **Official horizon definitions and review frequencies:** Purpose and Vision are reviewed "whenever additional clarity… is needed" and at "annual revisiting." Goals/Objectives (12 to 24 months) are reviewed "Annually; quarterly reviews and recalibrations." Areas ("standards to 'keep the engines running'") get "monthly personal check-in's." Projects (multi-action outcomes "completed within a year") get the "Weekly Review." Ground/Actions are reviewed "multiple times daily." — [GTD Horizons PDF](https://gettingthingsdone.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/2016-Levels-of-Your-Work.pdf)
- **GTD worked example of the full chain:** purpose ("assist others in achieving their dreams"), then vision ("world-class athlete and spokesperson"), then goal ("starting line-up position on a national team"), then area of focus ("rigorous training program"), then project ("get a new personal trainer"), then next action ("call your college coach"). — [GTD Horizons PDF](https://gettingthingsdone.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/2016-Levels-of-Your-Work.pdf)
- **Full Focus numeric cascade:** 8 Annual Goals, then ≤3 per quarter ("Quarterly Big 3"), then Weekly Big 3 (set in the Weekly Preview), then Daily Big 3. — [Full Focus: System](https://fullfocus.co/system/)
- **12WY cascade:** vision, then 1 to 3 twelve-week goals, then weekly tactics (lead), then weekly execution score (target ≥85%). — [LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/product-management/12-week-year-approach); [Goodreads 12WY notes](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/18961505-the-12-week-year/153805008-javy?page=2)
- **Newport's multi-scale planning:** quarterly, then weekly, then daily time-blocks, plus a separate values document. — [Video highlight: multi-scale planning](https://videohighlight.com/v/ZHjK2XxcjQk)
- **Research support for decomposition:** Latham & Seijts (1999), using a business game, found that "do-your-best goals were more effective than distal goals, but when proximal outcome goals were set in addition to the distal outcome goal, self-efficacy and profits were significantly higher." Also: "performance errors on a dynamic task are often due to deficient decomposition of a distal goal into proximal goals." — [Locke & Latham 2002](https://goal-lab.psych.umn.edu/orgpsych/2020/readings/5.%20Motivation/Locke%20&%20Latham%20(2002).pdf)
- **Areas as maintenance standards:** GTD H2 formats include "personal lifestyle checklists." BuJo asks during migration whether each recurring task is still needed. — [GTD Horizons PDF](https://gettingthingsdone.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/2016-Levels-of-Your-Work.pdf); [BuJo: How to](https://bulletjournal.com/pages/how-to-bullet-journal)
- **Rituals belong in the Ideal Week:** the template exists to "Block off times to get work done," "Create themes for different days of the week," and "Embed rituals into your week." — [Full Focus: Ideal Week](https://fullfocus.co/idealweek/)
- **Lead measures are the habit layer in 12WY:** workouts and sales calls are lead measures for weight and revenue lag measures. — [Goodreads 12WY notes p.2](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/18961505-the-12-week-year/153805008-javy?page=2)
- **Implementation intentions** ("if-then" plans for when, where and how) gave a meta-analytic effect of d ≈ 0.65 on goal attainment (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology* 38:69–119; 94 independent tests). — [KOPS Uni Konstanz record](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/2e749bfb-8533-437c-8203-7e788c910c5f); effect size per secondary source [GoalsAndProgress](https://goalsandprogress.com/implementation-intentions-gollwitzer-how-to/)

### Inferences
- **Proposed unified hierarchy (each level, its source framework and its review cadence):**
  1. **Purpose & Values** (GTD H5; Newport's values doc). Free text. Reviewed annually or on demand.
  2. **Vision, 3 to 5 years** (GTD H4). One statement per Life Area, optional. Reviewed annually.
  3. **Life Areas** (Wheel of Life = GTD H2 = PARA Areas). About 6 to 10 areas, e.g., Company/Work, Health, Relationships/Family, Finances, Growth/Learning, Fun, Home/Environment, Spirit. Each has a "standard" (what "maintained" means), a 1 to 10 satisfaction rating from the Wheel, and attached routines. Reviewed monthly: re-rate the wheel and check standards.
  4. **Annual Goals** (Full Focus; GTD H3). Up to about 8, each linked to one Area. Fields: SMARTER statement, ranked motivations, celebration. Reviewed annually, recalibrated quarterly.
  5. **Quarterly / 12-week Goals** (Full Focus Quarterly Big 3; 12WY; OKR). Enforced ≤3 active. Fields: objective, 1 to 3 key results (lag, graded 0 to 1.0), and lead-measure tactics with weekly targets. Scored weekly (execution %) and graded at quarter end (KR %).
  6. **Projects** (GTD H1 = PARA Projects). Multi-step outcomes with an end state. Each links to exactly one Area and optionally one Goal (projects with no goal are "maintenance projects"). Kanban status (backlog/active/waiting/done) with an active-WIP cap. Reviewed weekly.
  7. **Tasks / Next Actions** (GTD Ground). Context, energy, time estimate, scheduled/due, waiting-for. Optional "tactic" link to a goal lead measure. Reviewed daily.
  8. **Weekly plan** (Full Focus Weekly Big 3; 12WY weekly plan). 3 weekly outcomes plus the week's tactic instances, laid onto an Ideal Week template.
  9. **Daily plan** (Daily Big 3 / Ivy Lee / 1-3-5 / frog). User picks a "daily shape" (3, 6, or 1+3+5) and the items are placed into time blocks (Newport). Planned the evening before or at day start, with end-of-day carry-forward (Ivy Lee rule) and shutdown.
  10. **Logs and Reviews** (BuJo rapid log; GTD Weekly Review; Full Focus previews). The daily log captures events and notes. Weekly, monthly, quarterly and annual review records store scores and reflections and roll up.
- **Habits and routines** should be one "Recurring Commitment" object with two parents. It is either (a) an *Area standard* (maintenance, e.g., "lift 3x/week" under Health), whose misses lower the Area's health score, or (b) a *Goal lead measure* (12WY tactic), which counts toward the weekly execution score. Routines (morning, shutdown, weekly review) are scheduled blocks inside the Ideal Week template. Routines are framed as "if-then" plans (implementation intentions) because the evidence base for that wording is strong.
- **Goal-less work is legitimate.** Most of a founder's tasks will belong to Areas (ops, admin, health maintenance) and not to quarterly goals. Forcing every task to roll up to a goal would contradict GTD H2 and invite fake goals.
- **Founder-specific tweak:** the company is one Area (or several work Areas such as Product, Fundraising, Hiring). Company OKRs can later become the parent of the founder's work-area quarterly goals, which gives the "scale to a small company" path without changing the hierarchy.

### Gaps
- No source empirically validates any specific multi-level hierarchy (e.g., that 3 to 5 year vision documents improve outcomes). The hierarchy rests on practitioner consensus plus proximal-goal research.
- Hyatt's habit-goal vs. achievement-goal distinction and streak rules could not be verified from a primary page in this session.
- I found no research on the optimal number of life areas. The 8-area convention is just that, a convention.

## Q4. What are the documented failure modes (system overload, over-planning, "productivity porn", abandoned systems, too many goals) and the remedies?

### Takeaway
The best-documented failure is abandonment through review lapse: when the weekly review stops, the system goes stale, trust collapses and people "fall off the wagon." David Allen calls this almost universal. Next come tinkering with the system instead of doing the work ("productivity porn," Merlin Mann's "Rube Goldberg device"), overcommitment/pseudo-productivity (Newport) and too many simultaneous goals (Full Focus warns of burnout above 3 per quarter). Remedies with support: fixed short review slots and a catch-up review after a lapse, hard caps on active goals and WIP, scoring execution rather than outcomes, and limiting time spent on the system itself.

### Cited Findings
- **Abandonment through review lapse:** Allen says partial implementation leaves commitments in your head, so you don't trust the system, you get driven by the "latest and loudest," and keeping the system current stops seeming worth the effort. He calls falling off "almost universal." — [David Allen Substack (June 2025)](https://davidallen.substack.com/p/partial-or-complete-gtd)
- A David Allen blog post (via a search summary) recounts that skipping the review for more than three weeks led to falling off the wagon, and calls dropping the review the surest way to fail at GTD. — [Goodreads (David Allen blog): Getting (back?) on the wagon](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/15358466-getting-back-on-the-wagon-with-gtd)
- "Every GTDer has put off the weekly review, sometimes for several weeks." — [Zen Habits](https://zenhabits.net/weekly-review-key-to-gtd-and-achieving/)
- **Remedies from practitioners:** find a weekly time when the review feels easy; after a long lapse, do an extra-long catch-up review (or restart the setup); any review is better than none. — [GTD forum: GTD gone off track](https://forum.gettingthingsdone.com/threads/gtd-gone-off-track.12401); [GTD forum: back on the wagon](https://forum.gettingthingsdone.com/threads/how-to-get-back-on-the-wagon.14762/latest). Reviews often fail because people treat them as fixed and abandon them instead of adapting them. — [Asian Efficiency: why your weekly review keeps failing](https://www.asianefficiency.com/habits/why-your-weekly-review-keeps-failing-and-10-ways-to-fix-it/)
- **"Productivity porn" and system tinkering:** a forum post credits the term "productivity pr0n" to Merlin Mann, who later described feeling "tossed around by a menacing Rube Goldberg device" of his own design while refining his system. The "'system' becomes a goal in itself." — [Obsidian forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/how-do-you-manage-pornographic-productivity-in-obsidian/15906/20); [Cursor (TU Eindhoven) opinion](https://www.cursor.tue.nl/en/opinion/leander-van-eekelen/productivity-porn). One suggested remedy is to confine productivity-content consumption to a scheduled block. — [Todoist: productivity addiction](https://www.todoist.com/de/inspiration/productivity-addiction)
- **Over-complexity:** Forte warns that a system as complex as your life will drain the time you need to live it, and keeps PARA to four folders with "a small number of subfolders." His onboarding remedy is to archive everything into a dated folder instead of sorting the backlog. — [Forte Labs: PARA](https://fortelabs.com/blog/para/); [Forte Labs: BASB](https://fortelabs.com/blog/basboverview/)
- **Overload / pseudo-productivity:** Newport says knowledge workers usually control their own workload with no oversight, keep saying yes, and confuse activity with useful work. His remedy is "do fewer things" through explicit workload management (quotas, active vs. waiting). — [Cal Newport: Slow Productivity](https://calnewport.com/my-new-book-slow-productivity/); [Cal Newport: Workload Fairy Tale](https://calnewport.com/the-workload-fairy-tale/)
- **Too many goals:** Full Focus caps quarterly goals at 3 because more "risks burnout." 12WY uses 1 to 3 goals per 12 weeks. OKRs cap at 3 to 5 objectives. — [Full Focus: System](https://fullfocus.co/system/); [LogRocket: 12WY](https://blog.logrocket.com/product-management/12-week-year-approach); [Shortform: MWM](https://shortform.com/summary/measure-what-matters-summary-john-doerr). One GTD+12WY practitioner caps weekly tactics at 14. — [GTD forum](https://forum.gettingthingsdone.com/posts/145854)
- **Too much WIP:** Personal Kanban's remedy is a WIP limit of about 3 and pulling new work only after finishing. — [iSixSigma](https://www.isixsigma.com/kanban-tool/personal-kanban/); [TasksBoard](https://tasksboard.com/blog/personal-kanban-board)
- **Brittle plans / over-planning:** Newport builds in room to re-plan blocks mid-day. — [Cal Newport](https://calnewport.com/deep-habits-the-importance-of-planning-every-minute-of-your-work-day/). Ideal Week practitioners warn that "open" calendar gaps are usually underestimated tasks. — [Full Focus: 3 keys to crafting your Ideal Week](https://fullfocus.co/3-keys-to-crafting-your-ideal-week-to-win-back-your-time/). Research (Masicampo & Baumeister, *Social Cognition* 2012, "Committed but closed-minded") found that a fixed plan can reduce attention to alternatives and keep people on the original plan after obstacles make it unwise. — [University of Mannheim summary](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/forschung-erleben/artikel/lieber-planlos-als-verplant/)
- **Wrong priority chosen:** the main failure of Eat the Frog is "picking the wrong frog." — [Griply](https://griply.app/guides/productivity/eat-the-frog-meaning-the-brian-tracy-method-explained). The main critique of Ivy Lee-style methods is that they are "too basic." — [Fabric: Ivy Lee](https://fabric.so/learn/ivy-lee-method)
- **Wrong goal type for complex/novel work:** on complex tasks, specific performance-outcome goals can create "evaluative pressure and performance anxiety." People "scramble to discover strategies in an unsystematic way." The antidote is "specific challenging learning goals," such as discovering a set number of strategies. — [Locke & Latham 2002](https://goal-lab.psych.umn.edu/orgpsych/2020/readings/5.%20Motivation/Locke%20&%20Latham%20(2002).pdf)
- **Individual-only systems hitting organizational limits:** Newport (via Farkas) argues that GTD-style personal systems fail because overload is an organizational problem. — [Meredith Farkas](https://meredithfarkas.substack.com/p/the-productivity-trap)

### Inferences
- **Product guardrails these sources imply:**
  - Hard defaults: ≤3 active quarterly goals, ≤3 Weekly Big 3, ≤3 Daily Big 3 (or user-chosen 6 / 1-3-5), ≤3 to 5 active projects per Area or overall (Kanban WIP).
  - Overflow goes to Someday/Maybe or a goal backlog, never silently into "active."
  - A weekly review that is short by default (10 to 20 minutes, guided, pre-filled), with a "catch-up review" mode after a missed week. It should track a review streak and quietly flag when the system's data is going stale (e.g., tasks untouched for 30+ days, projects without a next action).
  - An "anti-tinkering" stance: few user-configurable taxonomies, sensible templates, one capture inbox. Measure time spent in planning views against time spent doing.
  - Score execution (lead) weekly and outcomes (lag) quarterly. That way a bad outcome week doesn't break motivation, and a good execution score still shows progress.
  - BuJo-style migration prompts for stale tasks (keep/schedule/drop) to stop list bloat, which is the digital equivalent of "not worth rewriting."
  - Learning goals as an option for novel founder work (e.g., "run 10 pricing experiments" rather than "hit $X MRR"), in line with Locke & Latham.
  - Re-plan affordance: one-tap reshuffle of today's blocks when the day breaks, as Newport does.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data was found on abandonment rates for any productivity system or app (e.g., the percentage of GTD adopters still practicing after 6 or 12 months).
- Merlin Mann's original "productivity pr0n" writing was not retrieved. The attribution rests on a forum post.
- No controlled study was found that compares time blocking with to-do lists, or that tests the 12WY 85% threshold. The 85% figure is the authors' own observation.

## Q5. Is there evidence (studies or credible practitioner data) on what works — goal-setting theory, and the cost of too many concurrent goals?

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence is Locke & Latham's goal-setting theory: specific, difficult goals beat "do your best" (meta-analytic d ≈ 0.42–0.80), and the effect depends on commitment, feedback and task complexity. Proximal sub-goals plus feedback improve results on dynamic tasks. Implementation intentions add a medium-to-large effect (d ≈ 0.65), and written plans for unfinished goals reduce intrusive thoughts. Evidence on concurrent goals is real but messier: goal conflict is linked to lower well-being and performance, people typically hold 10 to 15 personal goals, and the meta-analytic picture is mixed. Framework-specific numbers (85%, 0.7, Big 3) are practitioner heuristics, not research findings.

### Cited Findings
- **Core finding:** "We found a positive, linear function in that the highest or most difficult goals produced the highest levels of effort and performance. Goal difficulty effect sizes (d) in meta-analyses ranged from .52 to .82." Performance "leveled off or decreased only when the limits of ability were reached or when commitment to a highly difficult goal lapsed." — [Locke & Latham 2002, American Psychologist 57(9):705–717](https://goal-lab.psych.umn.edu/orgpsych/2020/readings/5.%20Motivation/Locke%20&%20Latham%20(2002).pdf)
- **Specific-hard vs. "do your best":** "specific, difficult goals consistently led to higher performance than urging people to do their best. The effect sizes in meta-analyses ranged from .42 to .80." — [Locke & Latham 2002](https://goal-lab.psych.umn.edu/orgpsych/2020/readings/5.%20Motivation/Locke%20&%20Latham%20(2002).pdf)
- **Four mechanisms:** goals (1) direct attention toward goal-relevant activities and away from irrelevant ones, (2) energize effort, (3) increase persistence ("Tight deadlines lead to a more rapid work pace than loose deadlines"), and (4) prompt the discovery and use of task strategies. A notable directive-function finding: people given feedback on several aspects of a driving task improved "on the dimensions for which they had goals but not on other dimensions." — [Locke & Latham 2002](https://goal-lab.psych.umn.edu/orgpsych/2020/readings/5.%20Motivation/Locke%20&%20Latham%20(2002).pdf)
- **Moderators:**
  - *Commitment:* "The goal–performance relationship is strongest when people are committed to their goals… Commitment is most important and relevant when goals are difficult." It is raised by the goal's importance and by self-efficacy.
  - *Feedback:* "the combination of goals plus feedback is more effective than goals alone."
  - *Task complexity:* goal-difficulty effect sizes are smaller on complex tasks (d = .48 for the most complex vs. .67 for the least; specific-hard vs. do-your-best d = .41 vs. .77).
  - *Learning vs. performance goals:* on complex tasks, specific challenging *learning* goals beat both performance goals and do-your-best (Winters & Latham 1996; Seijts & Latham 2001).
  - *Proximal goals:* distal-plus-proximal goals produced higher self-efficacy and profits than distal-only or do-your-best (Latham & Seijts 1999).
  — [Locke & Latham 2002](https://goal-lab.psych.umn.edu/orgpsych/2020/readings/5.%20Motivation/Locke%20&%20Latham%20(2002).pdf)
- **Goal conflict:** "Goal conflict undermines performance if it motivates incompatible action tendencies" (Locke, Smith, Erez & Schaffer 1994). Aligned personal and group goals improve group performance; misaligned ones hurt it. — [Locke & Latham 2002](https://goal-lab.psych.umn.edu/orgpsych/2020/readings/5.%20Motivation/Locke%20&%20Latham%20(2002).pdf)
- **Multiple personal goals are normal, and conflict is costly:** people typically list 10 to 15 personal goals (Little 1983), so multiple-goal pursuit is "the rule rather than the exception." Goal conflict is "generally associated with poorer performance" and lower well-being (Emmons 1986; Emmons & King 1988; Brunstein 1993). Conflicting alternatives cause motivational interference "even when the dismissed alternatives are only mentally present" (Fries et al. 2008). Self-concordant goals are more likely to be felt as challenge than strain. The literature is "scattered," and heterogeneous designs limit meta-analysis (Gray, Ozer & Rosenthal 2017). — [Gorges & Grund 2017, Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.02011/full)
- **Meta-analysis figure (unverified):** a secondary blog reports that Gray, Ozer & Rosenthal (2017, *J. Research in Personality* 66:27–37) found goal conflict associated with distress at r = .34 across 54 studies. I could not verify this against the paper. Later authors describe the conflict and well-being findings as divergent. — [GoalsAndProgress (secondary)](https://goalsandprogress.com/managing-conflicting-priorities-framework-success/); [ECNU Journal of Psychological Science 2023](https://jps.ecnu.edu.cn/EN/Y2023/V46/I6/1391)
- **Open loops and plan-making (supports GTD capture and clarify):** unfinished goals caused intrusive thoughts, higher accessibility of goal words and poorer anagram performance. Making specific plans eliminated these effects, mediated by how earnest the plans were. "Once a plan is made, the drive to attain a goal is suspended… and is resumed at the specified later time." — Masicampo & Baumeister 2011, *JPSP* 101(4):667–683, via [Mind Blog summary](https://mindblog.dericbownds.net/2010/11/brain-clutter-whats-left-undone-lingers.html?m=1) and [Scrapbox notes](https://scrapbox.io/nishio/consider_it_done). Caveat from the same authors (2012): fixed plans can make people closed-minded to alternatives. — [Uni Mannheim](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/forschung-erleben/artikel/lieber-planlos-als-verplant/)
- **Implementation intentions:** Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006) meta-analysis, d = 0.65 on goal attainment across 94 independent tests. The effect size is reported by secondary sources, and the bibliographic record is confirmed. — [KOPS Uni Konstanz](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/2e749bfb-8533-437c-8203-7e788c910c5f); [GoalsAndProgress](https://goalsandprogress.com/implementation-intentions-gollwitzer-how-to/); [The Behavioral Scientist glossary](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/glossary/implementation-intentions)
- **Debunked claim to avoid in product copy:** the "1953 Yale study" (3% of graduates with written goals out-earned the other 97%) never happened, per a Fast Company investigation cited secondarily. — [Tability: Goal setting theory](https://www.tability.io/odt/articles/goal-setting-theory)
- **Practitioner data:** the 12WY authors' 85% execution threshold comes from their own client observation, not a study. — [Goodreads 12WY notes](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/18961505-the-12-week-year/153805008-javy?page=2). Newport's "40 hours time-blocked ≈ 60+ unstructured" is an unsupported personal estimate. — [Cal Newport](https://calnewport.com/deep-habits-the-importance-of-planning-every-minute-of-your-work-day/). The often-cited "Dominican University study" (43% vs. 76% goal attainment with written goals, commitments and weekly accountability reports) appears only in secondary blogs and I could not verify it here. — [Yu Wenhao: 12WY guide](https://yu-wenhao.com/en/blog/12-week-year-guide/)

### Inferences
- **Evidence-backed design rules for the app:**
  - Goals must be specific and quantified, not "do your best." Prompt for a number or date.
  - Every goal gets a feedback surface: progress against a KR and weekly execution score. The research says goals without feedback underperform.
  - Decompose distal goals (annual) into proximal ones (quarterly, weekly). The proximal-goal findings directly support the 12WY / Full Focus cascade.
  - Offer "learning goal" framing for novel or complex founder work.
  - Commitment matters most for hard goals. Capture "why" (Full Focus's ranked motivations) and keep the count low so commitment isn't spread thin.
  - Detect goal conflict: at goal-setting time, ask the user to rate pairwise conflicts or resource overlaps (Emmons-style matrix) among active goals, and warn when two active goals compete for the same time blocks.
  - Capture plus a scheduled "when" for each open loop (inbox, then next action, then scheduled block or review date) is supported by Masicampo & Baumeister's plan-making findings and by implementation intentions.
- **The "≤3 active goals" cap is a practitioner convention, consistent with the research but not directly derived from it.** The research shows conflict and interference costs and that attention narrows to goal dimensions (so unmeasured areas may suffer). It does not pin down an optimal number. An Area-health check (Wheel of Life ratings) is a reasonable counterweight to the narrowing effect.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the original Gray, Ozer & Rosenthal (2017) meta-analysis, so its effect size (reported as r = .34) is unverified.
- I found no peer-reviewed study testing any branded system (GTD, PARA, BuJo, Full Focus, 12WY) as a whole. Heylighen & Vidal (2008) on GTD's cognitive-science basis was not retrieved in this session.
- I found no research directly testing time blocking, Ivy Lee, 1-3-5 or Eat the Frog against alternatives.
- The Matthews (Dominican University) written-goals study was not verified from a primary source.
