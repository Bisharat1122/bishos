## 1. Core philosophy (recurring across videos)

- **Data-enabled creativity.** Use data (outliers) to pick topics, hooks and formats. Save creativity for the contrarian insight. He uses "de-risk" for this: more hits per rep. Sources: pirnGDCZD3Y [V], Creator Science [V2].
- **Hooks are the 80/20.** He calls hooks the 80 of the 80/20 in content. Sources: xnOe8aA9Pmw [V], 2byPP_9F0-Q [V2].
- **Idea is the most important part.** "Nothing else matters if the idea is weak." Source: pirnGDCZD3Y [V].
- **Packaging is the most slept-on part.** Source: jZCupmAOMAE [V].
- **Audience matching.** Keep one topic and one avatar per channel, so the algorithm learns who to send each video to. Sources: 8cQidXgtGmU, h_yQswsXwhY, 3y-WiiUaqb4 [V].
- **Views are not money.** Value is captured at the offer layer, so every video needs a ramp to an owned asset (email). Sources: h_yQswsXwhY, pSIY9ngEZC8 [V], Creator Science [V2].
- **Term branding.** He names every framework. Examples: LEGO bricks, content minutes, audience matching, trust ladder, storylocks. Source: pcnrzBwoVUk [V].

## 2. HOOKS

His hook teaching has changed over time (2024 to 2026). All versions are listed below. Section 2.11 gives a consolidated model for the app.

### 2.1 The 3-Step Hook Formula (his "mega tip") [V] (LmXpbP7dD48)
A hook builds a **curiosity loop**: each line makes the viewer need the next one. Length is 2 to 4 sentences.
1. **Context Lean-in.** Give the topic in the first one or two lines so viewers can opt in or out. Then make them lean in by doing one of these:
   - establish common ground,
   - reference a benefit or pain point,
   - use a metaphor for a complex idea,
   - state a mind-blowing fact.
2. **Scroll-Stop Interjection.** One line that stuns. It usually starts with a contrast word: but, however, yet, although, therefore, on the other hand. It sets up the next line.
3. **Contrarian Snapback.** A line that is still on topic but turns opposite to the initial lean. The bigger the shock, the stronger the snap.
- His example (8M views): line 1 says the Vegas Sphere's screen is huge, line 2 says the screen is the least impressive part, line 3 says the audio is the real story.
- Business template: "There are [N] mistakes [audience] make with [X]. Most people think it's because of [common belief]. It turns out that has nothing to do with it, because the biggest cost comes from..."
- Generic shape: if you want X, don't do Y, do Z. Z must actually pay off.

### 2.2 Five "sauce" add-ons to the 3-step formula [V] (LmXpbP7dD48)
1. **Visual hook.** Put 3 to 5 words of bold on-screen text that compress the context lean. Use one compelling visual with "just enough" motion: too much overwhelms, too little bores.
2. **Benefit-first context.** Lead with the viewer's benefit or pain, then the subject. Example shape: "If you want better sleep, you need X" beats "X is a mineral that...".
3. **Cult hopping.** Wrap a niche idea in a known brand, celebrity or cultural reference to create familiarity.
4. **Compress speed-to-value.** The first hit of value should land in about 4 seconds on short-form and within 1 to 2 minutes on YouTube. Front-load value, then alternate context and value.
5. **Staccato sentences in the hook.** Use short sentences up front for clarity and value per word. Vary length later in the video.

### 2.3 Hook alignment: Triple Hook / 4 hook components [V] (xnOe8aA9Pmw, pNIYikmYsyw, onQoYdxWXdA, jZCupmAOMAE)
- Components:
  - **Spoken hook**: what you say.
  - **Visual hook**: what is shown and how.
  - **Text hook**: title text on screen, not the captions.
  - **Audio hook**: music and SFX (named in xnOe8aA9Pmw).
  - **Caption hook**: first line of the caption above the fold (named in pNIYikmYsyw).
- **Rule: maximum alignment.** All components must mean the same thing. Each should predict the others. He claims this is the difference between roughly 500 and 500K views, and that misalignment is the most common client mistake.
- **Viewer intake order is Visual, then Audio, then Visual.** The eye takes in the visual and text first, then the ear catches the spoken hook, then the eye looks back for confirmation. Eyes process far faster than ears.
- **Key Visual first** (xnOe8aA9Pmw). Before writing, find the visual you can put in the first 3 to 5 seconds. If there is no good visual, either manufacture one (motion graphics, stock) or drop the idea.
- **5-step hook process** (xnOe8aA9Pmw):
  1. Pick the key visual.
  2. List the most interesting facts and angles, and choose the archetype with the biggest contrast.
  3. Write a 2 to 4 line spoken hook using lean, contrast, snapback.
  4. Add a text overlay that supports the visual.
  5. Watch it back with the Visual, Audio, Visual intake in mind. If comprehension is not maximal, rework it before moving on.

### 2.4 The 6 Hook Archetypes [V] (xnOe8aA9Pmw)
All six create **contrast** (A = what the viewer believes now, B = what you show; contrast is the distance between them) within about 5 seconds.

| Archetype | Contrast mechanism | Steps | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fortune Teller** | Present vs future | (a) current reality, (b) how the future changes, (c) tease that future | news, product innovation, authority on the future |
| **Experimenter** | Peer-to-peer demo or experiment | (a) base pain, (b) show you tried a new method or tool, (c) why it works better | product demos, B2B tools, new frameworks |
| **Teacher** | Teacher-to-student lesson (you or someone solved X with Y) | (a) pain, (b) your learnings or method, (c) why the steps work | building expert authority |
| **Magician** | Visual or verbal "stun gun"; stacks with the other five | signature visual, sound or phrase (his was "check this out"); **visual pacifier** (passive activity like makeup or cup-stacking) | scroll-stop layer |
| **Investigator** | A secret or finding they don't know | tee up the secret, frame it against their not-knowing | leading-edge or research positioning (most of his own shorts) |
| **Contrarian** | Explicitly states the opposite of conventional wisdom | find what you believe that most don't, then say it directly | experts in crowded spaces |

- Any topic can be written in all six archetypes. He demonstrates this with a backpack example. Pick the archetype that best fits the key visual.

### 2.5 The 9 Proven Hook Formats (spoken hook) [V] (_Z11mjFh2zY)
Format, then when to use it. Example lines are paraphrased.
1. **Secret Reveal.** An unknown insight or future implication. Example shape: "[Company] just released [N] free [thing] that make [outcome] way faster." Use for news, releases, new tips.
2. **Case Study.** A subject got a result in an unexpected way. Example shape: "[Person] just did [result] in [time] using this [N]-step method." Very strong for educators.
3. **Comparison.** A vs B, ideal vs suboptimal. Example shape: "Beginner creator vs experienced creator."
4. **Question.** Explicitly plants the question. Example shape: "Can you do X in under [time] using only Y?"
5. **Education.** Opens a step-by-step for one task. Example shape: "Here's the easiest way to use [tool] to [task]." Precedes a tutorial.
6. **List.** An ordered set of items. Example shape: "These are the [N] best [things] for [goal]."
7. **Contrarian.** A bold take in the first sentence. Drives comments.
8. **Personal Experience.** A first-person story setup. Example shape: "When I was [context], I used [technique] to [result]."
9. **Problem.** Agitates a known pain, then promises a solution. Example shape: "If you struggle with [pain], this is one obvious fix."
- Each niche usually has 1 or 2 formats that consistently win. Find them from outliers, then remix them Mad Lib style.
- In pNIYikmYsyw [V2] he says about a dozen formats outperform. His own top three were tutorial, secret reveal (breakdown), and problem.

### 2.6 The 6 Hook Power Words (hook anatomy) [V] (S9FlxFv9dxg)
Core four (required):
1. **Subject clarity word.** I, we, you, or a proper noun.
2. **Action word.** A verb.
3. **Objective / end-state word.** The result. The more shocking and non-obvious, the better.
4. **Contrast word.** Base state vs new state. It often overlaps with the objective, e.g. going from 0 to 100K.

Optional two:
5. **Proof word.** Example: adding "again". Needed more for education than entertainment.
6. **Time word.** Example: "in 5 months" or "in the next 90 days". Adds urgency and speed-to-result.

- Proof and time can move to lines 2 and 3.
- Practice method, **"copy work"**:
  - Pull top hooks from niche outliers.
  - Label the six buckets in each.
  - Swap in your topic.
  - Do 10 to 30 a week.

### 2.7 The 4 S's of hook writing + text-hook rules + Lock-in Zone [V2, spot-checked] (pNIYikmYsyw, 2026)
- **Interrupt theory.** The hook must create a "freeze point" (the thumb stops for at least one second) through an orienting response.
- **4 S's** (all required):
  - **Subject**: who or what, instantly.
  - **Stakes**: why care. In education this is the desired outcome; in entertainment it is a curiosity scenario.
  - **Speed**: compression, not fast talking. Fewer words, e.g. about 8 rather than 28.
  - **Clarity**: only one possible interpretation, no wordplay.
- In this video **the text hook is the most important component and the visual hook the least**. This reverses older videos; see §17.
- **Text hook rules:**
  - Plain system font or a premium typeface.
  - Placed about one third from the top, never covering the face.
  - On screen for about 3 seconds.
  - At most 2 lines (3 only with premium typography).
  - Natural line breaks.
  - No punctuation except quotes or parentheses.
  - No connector words (and, or, that, but, therefore).
  - Formula: **subject repeater** (the proper noun or jargon) plus one of: pain point reminder, dream outcome teaser, or future state change.
  - Use specific numbers, power words, and a knowledge gap.
- **Spoken hook levels:**
  - Level 1 "hook maxing": extract and remix the formats that perform best.
  - Level 2 "power word remixing": reuse power phrases that transfer across niches, e.g. "machine", "impossible to skip/ignore".
- **Visual hook categories:**
  1. graphic and text overlays,
  2. pattern interrupts / visual switching,
  3. subject motion in or out of frame,
  4. VFX or transitions,
  5. visual selection.
  - Principle: motion, color, contrast, aligned with the other components. Start simple: move yourself, the visual, or the text.
- **Lock-in Zone.** The 5 to 10 seconds (2 to 4 sentences) after the hook. Jobs: (1) confirm the hook claim (no bait-and-switch), (2) let the viewer evaluate trust quickly.
  - Four levers: appearance, packaging quality (set, light, audio), relatability (looks like the expected messenger), proof as a "trust anchor".

### 2.8 The 4 hook failure modes [V2, spot-checked] (2byPP_9F0-Q)
A winning hook needs **topic clarity** plus **on-target curiosity**. Hooks fail for four reasons:
1. **Delay.** The topic arrives late. Fix: topic within 1 to 2 seconds ("speed to value"); cut fluff openers like "this is the craziest thing I've seen".
2. **Confusion (comprehension loss).** Fix: fewer words, about a 6th-grade reading level, active voice. Tip: ask an LLM to rewrite at a 6th-grade level without changing meaning. Reread the hook alone and ask whether it can be misread.
3. **Irrelevance.** Fix: use "you/your" not "I/me". Frame as need-to-have (solve a specific problem) rather than nice-to-have.
4. **Disinterest.** Fix: a curiosity loop built on **contrast**.
   - **Stated contrast** names both sides, e.g. "most people do A, but B is 3x faster".
   - **Implied contrast** names only the new side, e.g. "this is 8x more effective".
- Structure: line 1 gives clarity, lines 2 and 3 give contrast.

### 2.9 Killer Hook: 4 mistakes + 7-step hook checklist + final audit [V2, spot-checked] (onQoYdxWXdA)
Mistakes:
1. Not a single subject with a single question.
2. Visual, spoken and text hooks misaligned (he says he pioneered the three-hook concept).
3. No scroll-stopping visual in the first 2 seconds. His **"minimum viable visual"** needs one of:
   - an attractive or unique-looking person,
   - a recognizable person or brand,
   - an atypical visual for the category,
   - an atypical layout.
   Upgrades: in-world set or green screen, B-roll, AI visuals. Keep a thumb-stop swipe folder.
4. Not data-driven. Mine your own winning hooks first, then small and mid creators in your niche. Target 5 to 10 reliable hook formats and a hit rate of 1 in 5 instead of 1 in 20.

**7-step hook checklist:**
1. Pick the subject.
2. Decide the question (emotional or shock-driven).
3. Envision the visual hook.
4. Write the spoken hook: 1 to 3 sentences, 5th-grade vocabulary, active voice, no extra words.
5. Gut check: one subject, one question.
6. Build the visual and text and align all three. Compare scroll-stop power with the niche.
7. Final audit, four questions:
   - Is the subject clear?
   - Is the question clear?
   - Are all three components aligned?
   - Am I curious enough to hear sentence two?

### 2.10 The 4 Hook Commandments (post-write filter) [V] (_Z11mjFh2zY)
1. **Alignment** of visual, spoken and text.
2. **Speed to value**: zero delay.
3. **Clarity**: one sentence makes the subject clear.
4. **Curiosity**: it opens a question.

### 2.11 Consolidated hook model for the app [I]
- **Input:** topic, audience, key visual.
- **Pick:** archetype (2.4) or format (2.5).
- **Build lines:** use the 3-step formula (2.1), check the 6 power-word slots (2.6), then write the text hook (2.7) and plan the visual (2.3).
- **Run filters:**
  - 4 commandments (2.10),
  - 4 failure modes (2.8),
  - 7-step audit (2.9),
  - Lock-in Zone check (2.7).

## 3. STORYTELLING & RETENTION

### 3.1 Six storytelling techniques [V] (t5Z-Q1bg1tU, his most-viewed video)
1. **The Dance.** Alternate context and conflict. Link beats with **"but" or "therefore", never "and then"** (credited to the South Park creators). His Stanley Cup example has 4 conflict loops in the first 30 seconds.
2. **Rhythm.** Mix short, medium and long sentences (he cites Gary Provost). Write one sentence per line; the right edge of the script should look jagged.
3. **Tone.** Talk to one close friend. Tape a photo of them under the lens. Write as if texting them.
4. **Direction.** Write the ending first. The last line is the **"last dab"**: memorable enough to share, and in shorts it sets up the loop back to the first line. His process is to write the first and last lines, then fill the middle.
5. **Story Lenses.** Your unique angle on a common topic. Example: Taylor Swift at the Super Bowl. Common lenses were the outfit or reaction; less common was a prediction; his was the business impact on the NFL (about 1M views).
6. **Hook.** The first line is punchy and states the plot. Avoid opaque openers like "wait till you see this". "Show while you tell": he says visual hooks are about 10x more effective than audio-only.

### 3.2 Storylocks (6 word-level retention techniques) [V] (pcnrzBwoVUk)
1. **Term Branding.** Name your concepts. The labeling effect makes an unknown term create anticipation.
2. **Embedded Truths.** Remove hedges (if, maybe, might, could, probably). Use certainty instead: "when you...", "the reason this works...", "once you...".
3. **Thought Narration.** Voice what the viewer is thinking, e.g. "you're probably thinking...". This builds trust and personalization at scale. Use it after major points.
4. **Negative Frames.** Loss aversion: people are about 2x more motivated to avoid pain. "Negative flip" or inverting turns positive advice into a warning, e.g. "how to grow on YouTube" becomes "this is killing your YouTube growth".
5. **Loop Openers.** A re-hook phrase that "flips the attention hourglass". Examples: "but that's not even the most interesting part", "here's what nobody talks about". Cadence: **every 20 to 30 seconds in shorts and every 60 to 90 seconds in long-form.** Use about 3 or 4 for a 5-section video. The best ones contain a contrast word.
6. **Contrast Words.** The base layer under all other locks: but, actually, instead, turns out, except, yet. Split key sentences into A then B. Don't overuse it.

### 3.3 The Dopamine Ladder (6 levels) [V] (jtmstMt4WLc)
The goal is a Pavlovian effect: seeing your face or name alone triggers anticipation.
1. **Stimulation** (0 to 2 seconds). Color, motion, brightness, contrast. This is "bottom-up processing" (about 200 ms). Use a unique palette or aesthetic so you don't blend in. It is the weakest and shortest level.
2. **Captivation.** An open question pops in the viewer's head. Ask it outright or imply it. Test: what is the most curiosity-inducing question, and is it relevant to the avatar? Tools: contrast (known vs unknown) or a shocking premise.
3. **Anticipation.** The viewer guesses the answer. Give clues, then head-fake or misdirect just before the reveal. Facts must stay clear or anticipation dies.
4. **Validation.** Close the loop with a non-obvious payoff (the tip, the resolution).
5. **Affection.** Liking the messenger (hard for faceless content). Levers: attractiveness, vibe/dress/posture, smiling, and the strongest one, actually solving their problem.
6. **Revelation.** The viewer realizes you are a consistent source of value. This is much easier to reach with education than entertainment. It comes from non-obvious, differentiated value delivered repeatedly.
- Levels 1 to 4 are the message; levels 5 and 6 are the messenger. Use the ladder as a checklist.

### 3.4 The Addiction Loop (4 steps, repeating) [V] (KyC8r-zitVE)
Dopamine tracks prediction, not pleasure (slot machine vs vending machine).
1. **Stakes** = a character to root for + something at risk + urgency (a ticking clock).
   - Stakes only need to be personally relevant, not life-or-death. Example shape: "48 hours to fix the ad budget".
   - Before the story starts, the viewer must know who, what is at risk, and why now.
2. **Big Question.** Give enough specifics for the viewer to predict. Avoid vague teasers. Before a reveal, back up and add context so a prediction forms. Load one big question early.
3. **Head Fake** (prediction error, the biggest dopamine spike).
   - Step 1: work out what the viewer will predict.
   - Step 2: reveal something that contradicts it but is logically consistent with the clues (a good surprise vs a cheap random one).
4. **Re-hook.** Close one loop and open the next in the same breath, with no dead air (like a casino's time-per-hand). Connector phrases follow these patterns:
   - "which would have been great, except..."
   - "but here's the problem..."
   - "that's when I realized..."
   - "which is exactly why..."

### 3.5 Seven storytelling mistakes + modern arc [V] (wgXGimZvDa4)
1. **Using the classic bell-curve arc.** Use the **"Kallaway story arc"** instead:
   - Start at about 70/100 intensity.
   - First conflict spike (about 90/100) within the first 1 to 2 minutes.
   - Release to about 30/100, then peak again around 75/100.
   - Repeat peak and release every 2 to 5 minutes (long-form).
2. **Jumbling the W's.** Order: **What + Why, then Who + How (credibility), then When + Where** (color last). Don't open with scene-setting.
3. **No re-buy-in.** Re-hook with new stakes or a new pain right after solving the previous one.
4. **No villain.** Name an antagonist: a group, a method, Corporate America, an old way.
5. **Nothing to root for.** Make the hero relatable through common ground.
6. **No atomic sharability.** The story must compress into a tiny retellable unit (the "Paul Revere effect"). Say complex points twice: once for a colleague, once as a child-level metaphor.
7. **Not painting the picture.** Use visuals.

### 3.6 18 psychology tricks across 6 viewer checkpoints [V2] (g_6TIu7zHDw)
1. **Pain Acceptance**
   - One standard deviation away: target an adjacent goal tied to the 4 core pains (money, time, health, access).
   - Twisting the knife.
   - Audience of one: say "you" constantly.
   - Pain-solution gap: visualize it with gestures or graphics.
2. **Building Trust**
   - Proof: 1 or 2 rapid visuals.
   - Common ground.
   - Professional setup.
3. **Plan of Attack**
   - Point of difference (contrarian).
   - Structured numbered list.
4. **Likability**
   - Energy (record line by line).
   - Simplification.
   - Authenticity.
5. **Holding Attention**
   - Open loops ("the hunt"): lead with your best point and tease a better one later.
   - Rehooking.
   - Varied rhythm.
6. **Taking Action**
   - Close the loop: free info is enough, but paying is faster.
   - Unexpected surprise: overdeliver.
   - Recap before the CTA.

## 4. SCRIPTING

### 4.1 Long-form 5-step script process [V2 + transcript spot-check] (7I50PECz7SU)
Principle: **expectations vs reality.** Viewers stay when reality beats what they expected.
1. **Packaging.**
   - One-line idea targeting a pain or a "rabbit hole".
   - **Lock the title before scripting.**
   - A loose thumbnail concept is enough at this stage.
2. **Outline.**
   - Bullets, each with what, why, and how it fits the story.
   - Check that every point is unique. If not, research more or kill the video ("measure twice, cut once").
3. **Intro formula.**
   1. Context / click confirmation.
   2. **Common belief**, restated.
   3. **Contrarian take.**
   4. and 5. **Proof** and **Plan** (ordered list), in either order.
4. **Body.**
   - Order: **second-best point first, best point second**, then the rest descending toward a climax.
   - Each point runs a **value loop**: context, then application (how, with examples), then framing (why it matters).
   - Zoom in and out between big picture and tactics.
   - **Rehook between points.**
5. **Outro.**
   - Recap and remind them reality beat expectations.
   - End high ("free dessert").
   - Bonus: **native embed CTA**. Set up a pain in the outline, then offer the matching free resource as the fix.

### 4.2 Short-form 4-blocker script system [V] (_Z11mjFh2zY)
The four blockers are interestingness, hook, storytelling and engagement.
1. **Law of Interesting.**
   - Interestingness = shock value = the distance between what the viewer knows and what you tell them.
   - Score each fact with a **Shock Score from 1 to 100**: out of 100 people, how many haven't heard it?
   - Collect 5 to 10 high-shock true facts as bullets. His example anchored a video on one unbelievable claim and got 40M views in 5 days.
2. **Hook.** Choose one of the 9 formats (2.5) and pass the 4 commandments (2.10).
3. **Story structure.** The 7 winning structures (he lists 8 names):

| Structure | Outline blocks | Best hooks |
|---|---|---|
| Breakdown | hook, context/shock, what happened or how it works, why it matters, outro | secret reveal, question, comparison, contrarian |
| Newscaster | hook, full context, optional short take, outro | secret reveal, question, comparison, contrarian, problem |
| Case-study explainer | hook, framework (2 to 4 parts), outro | case study, question, comparison |
| Listicle | hook, 3 to 5 independent items, outro | list, problem |
| Problem-solver | hook, problem (agitate), solution, outro | problem, question, contrarian |
| Tutorial | hook, sequential steps toward one outcome, outro | education |
| Educational / personal story | hook, personal story (start, middle, end), outro | personal experience, problem, contrarian |

   - List vs tutorial: list items stand alone; tutorial steps depend on each other.
   - Case study is reflection or analysis, not how-to.
4. **Engagement (emotional transfer).** Pick one of 6 emotion buckets and write it at the top of the page:
   - awe / inspiration,
   - amusement / humor,
   - excitement / joy,
   - anger / outrage,
   - surprise / shock / curiosity,
   - sadness / empathy.

**His routine:**
1. Research shock facts.
2. Write the target emotion at the top.
3. Pick the hook format and write the hook.
4. Pick the story structure and outline it.
5. Slot facts in and add transitions.
6. Reread through the emotion filter.

**Final 4 questions:**
- Is it interesting?
- Is it as compressed as possible?
- Does the hook hook me on its own?
- What emotion do I feel at the end?

He says AI should get the draft to about 90%; he edits manually and dislikes end-to-end AI scripts. Creator Science gives the AI share as 75 to 90% [V2].

## 5. IDEAS / TOPIC SELECTION

### 5.1 Idea LEGO Bricks (7 components of a video idea) [V] (pirnGDCZD3Y)
1. **Topic**: a one-sentence description.
2. **Angle**: premise + take + key facts.
3. **Hook structure**: spoken + text + visual.
4. **Story structure**: breakdown, listicle, tutorial, etc.
5. **Visual format**: layout, e.g. green screen, POV vlog, faceless text.
6. **Key visuals**: A-roll, B-roll, animations.
7. **Audio**: music and SFX.

### 5.2 Idea Mining System (3 steps) [V] (pirnGDCZD3Y)
1. **Outlier research.**
   - **Outlier score = a video's views ÷ the channel's average views.** Creator Science adds that the average is a rolling 3 months before posting [V2].
2. **Analyze outliers** across the 7 bricks and look for patterns.
3. **Hold the winners, remix the rest** (e.g. hold 2, remix 5). He compares it to five-card poker: keep some cards, redraw the others.
   - His habit: he usually changes the angle, hook and visual format, and keeps the topic, some key visuals and the audio mood.

**Where to look (2x2 matrix):**
- Best: **same content type** (short-form video) on the **same or a different platform**.
- Different content types (tweets, blogs) only give a topic seed; the hook, structure and format don't transfer.

**Finding inspiration channels (4 ways):**
- your existing list,
- who a top creator follows,
- platform keyword search,
- a database search.
- Build a watchlist of **20 to 30** top niche accounts.

**Filters (his presets):**
- outlier score ≥ **5x**,
- engagement rate ≥ **5%** (screens out paid brand deals),
- posted date: 7 or 14 days for news niches, 3, 6 or 12 months for evergreen,
- sort by outlier score.
- Check daily, or 4 to 5 times a week.

**Idea doc layout:**
- **Daily inspiration** section: per video, the topic, angle, hook notes, key visuals, link, audio notes.
- **Long-term patterns** section: hook structures, series structures, visual formats.
- First filter: is this topic interesting to me at all?
- Each session reviews 20 to 30 videos and produces a validated shortlist.

### 5.3 Who to study: "Kallaway's paradox" [V] (o_n5ED5fE1I), [V2] (Creator Science)
- Big creators' performance is compressed: brand equity props up their duds. So **ignore accounts over 1M followers**.
- Study creators in these tiers:
  - micro: 10 to 50K,
  - small: 50 to 250K,
  - medium: 250K to 1M.
- Creator Science version: about 10K to 250K on Instagram, posted in the last 2 months, engagement ≥ 2%.
- A breakout from a small creator suggests the idea itself won.

### 5.4 Topic quality rules [V2] (Creator Science) + [V] (8cQidXgtGmU, h_yQswsXwhY)
- **Topic sourcing mix**: roughly 70 to 80% from data on what similar creators covered successfully, roughly 20% original tests.
- A strong topic is validated, tied to a real pain point, and carries a **non-obvious contrarian angle**.
- **Avatar filter**: ask whether the core avatar would find it interesting, useful, valuable or entertaining. If not, don't make it, even if it would go viral.
- **Idea source (older)**: he skimmed 10 to 12 curated email newsletters instead of about 30 news sites (Castmagic [V2]).
- **Research team cadence** (Creator Science [V2]):
  - A team member reviews outliers daily or every other day, using 7, 14 or 30-day windows.
  - Keepers are logged in a Google Doc, one tab per idea.
  - Pitch calls twice a week approve 8 to 10 ideas, which go to scripting.
- **Sandcastles "deep analysis" fields** (useful as an app schema) [V2]:
  - topic (3 to 5 words),
  - one-sentence description,
  - unique angle,
  - common belief vs contrarian reality,
  - core evidence,
  - hook formula and category,
  - storytelling structure,
  - visuals.

### 5.5 Workflow control model [V2] (Creator Science)
- Each stage (topic, research, hook, script, record, edit, post) can be anywhere from 0% to 100% data-driven.
- His mix: topic 100% data; contrarian substance 100% creativity; hooks and scripts data-informed.

## 6. PACKAGING (titles, thumbnails, short-form packaging)

### 6.1 Packaging definition + 10 tips [V] (jZCupmAOMAE)
- **YouTube packaging** = title + thumbnail + the first 30 to 60 seconds of the intro.
- **Short-form packaging** = first caption line + cover thumbnail + hook (first 3 to 5 seconds: visual, spoken, text).
- Analogy: a table of gifts. Packaging must earn (1) initial awareness, then (2) value-based curiosity.

YouTube tips:
1. **Invert the niche's color meta.** Dark and busy niches call for light and minimal thumbnails, and the reverse.
2. **Design for dark mode.** He says over 80% of viewers use it.
3. **Packaging psychology: Thumbnail, then Title, then Thumbnail, then click.**
   - Bottom-up: color and brightness catch the eye.
   - Then title keywords give a go / no-go.
   - Then top-down comprehension of the thumbnail confirms it.
   - Use this sequence to diagnose which step fails. Shorter titles win.
4. **Trust quality.** The viewer has a trust threshold from 1 to 100. Design quality (sharp, well-lit, readable) raises your score. Outsource design if you can't do it.
5. **Confirm the click.** Restate the title promise in the first lines. His took 4 seconds.

Short-form tips:

6. **Spoken hook clarity.** Read the first 1 or 2 lines alone. They must give the subject plus curiosity.
7. **Visual hook.** Raise color, motion and brightness, but don't strobe or disconnect from the message.
8. **Always use a title text hook.** Text is processed faster than speech.
9. **Custom short-form cover thumbnails.** These matter for shares into group DMs, profile binge sessions (a new fan watches 6 to 10 videos), and the look of the grid.
10. **Optimize the first caption line.** Write it like a title with a curiosity loop.

### 6.2 Thumbnail system [V2] (Iqy644uOOqM)
- **Viewer flow:**
  1. **Visual Stun Gun.**
  2. **Title Value Hunting.**
  3. **Visual Validation.**
- **7 stun-gun elements:**
  1. color science / contrast,
  2. large face with emotion (an unknown face can hurt beginners),
  3. compelling graphic,
  4. large text or numbers,
  5. red circles or arrows,
  6. aesthetic imagery,
  7. design collage.
- **Max 3 elements.** Never repeat the title in the thumbnail.
- **Titles:**
  - **Desire loop** (education): pain point blocking a want, e.g. money, growth, followers.
  - **Interest loop** (entertainment): an unanswered question.
  - Core test: do they want it, and does the thumbnail make them believe it will deliver?
- **5-step playbook:**
  1. Approach: treat the thumbnail as the most important asset; outsource.
  2. Define the desire loop and the pain point / solution.
  3. Concept with 3 composition types: symmetrical, asymmetrical (rule of thirds), A/B split.
  4. Design. Transformation graphics come in 4 kinds: end state, process, before/after, anti-transformation.
  5. Test at least 3 options with YouTube A/B testing. He made 7 options and tested the top 3.
- **Tactical rules:**
  - Green = good, red = bad.
  - Keep the bottom-right corner clear (timestamp overlay).
  - Do a 4K photo shoot with many emotions and poses.
  - Face emotion should match the title's tone.
  - Do a 1/16-size legibility check.
  - Use shadows for separation.
  - Clickbait is fine if you deliver.
  - Keep a swipe file.
  - Start the thumbnail early.
- **From 3y-WiiUaqb4 [V]:**
  - Have the title and thumbnail roughly dialed **before** making the video.
  - Use the 3-item rule.
  - Best-in-class CTR is about 10 to 12%.

## 7. ALGORITHM, PLATFORM & STRATEGY

### 7.1 How algorithms work [V] (8cQidXgtGmU)
- The platform's goal is session time, which means ad revenue. The algorithm is a **matchmaker**.
- **Pipeline:**
  1. Multimodal "digital fingerprint" (vision, audio transcript, metadata).
  2. **Topic mapping.**
  3. **Fit score.**
  4. **Initial sample of about 200 people**, mostly non-followers.
  5. Result: positive data expands roughly 2K, then 20K, then 200K; neutral data resamples another 200; negative data stops ("200 view jail").
- **Lever 1: audience matching.** Narrow topics and one avatar, consistently. One off-target viral hit can poison the next several videos.
- **Lever 2: sample engagement.** Three metrics:
  - average watch time / % completion,
  - engagement rate (likes + comments + shares ÷ views),
  - **watch-time session share** (not visible to creators).
- **4 engagement drivers:**
  1. relevant topic (solves their problem),
  2. non-obvious and tactically implementable,
  3. high absorption (understandable),
  4. short distance to implement.
- **5 comment drivers:**
  1. take a hard stance,
  2. take the contrarian side,
  3. amplify the framing,
  4. talk about cult-loved brands or people,
  5. drive strong emotion.
- **Myths he rejects:** posting time, hashtags and captions don't matter. The cake is great videos for one avatar on a narrow topic band.

### 7.2 Five uncommon learnings [V] (h_yQswsXwhY)
1. **Social media is now just media.** Matching is driven by transcript and caption. Make 30 to 50 videos on one pain-driven topic for one avatar.
2. **Pure virality is a trap.** Aim for **on-target virality**: maximum penetration of your narrow slice.
3. **One hero platform plus email, for 6 months.**
   - Video-native.
   - Reply to comments and DMs, comment in the niche.
   - Ask customers which platform they use.
4. **Islands, not ecosystems.**
   - Don't cross-promote platform to platform (links get suppressed, and consumption speeds differ).
   - Only ramp from rented to owned (email, community).
   - Use "world building": consistent visuals without forcing discovery.
5. **Value doesn't accrue at the media layer.** The stack is platform / media / offering. Use media to feed owned products.

### 7.3 Five Content Games [V] (o_n5ED5fE1I)
1. **Mainstream cult fandom.** Peak fame; ad and media revenue; you are the product.
2. **Category cult fandom.** Own one category; CPMs and brand deals.
3. **Intuitive products.** CPG or apparel that need no explanation; product conversions.
4. **Explanatory products.** Products needing explanation (e.g. a standing desk, a SaaS tool); single-session conversions.
5. **Authority building.** Narrow expertise; conversions to email or a low-ticket product, usually by direct attribution.
- You can win any game at any size, but **only one game per channel**.
- **Metric by game:**
  - Games 1 and 2: views, followers, engagement.
  - **Games 3 to 5: $ per view or conversions per view.**
- For a startup founder and company: the company channel is likely game 3 or 4, the founder brand likely game 5. [I]

### 7.4 Game theory of social media [V2] (o2nP0lfIsso)
- **Players:**
  1. you,
  2. the algorithm,
  3. passive viewers (no pain, never buy),
  4. active viewers (have the pain, may buy),
  5. entertainment creators,
  6. education creators,
  7. brands / advertisers.
- **Two games:**
  - **Awareness** (paid on CPM).
  - **Conversion** (paid on offers).
  - Only one game per channel.
- **Awareness attributes:**
  1. shareworthy (first or different, so the sharer gains social credit; a "super share" from a big account),
  2. curiosity loop in the hook,
  3. emotional payoff at the end,
  4. large TAM.
- **Conversion attributes:**
  1. on-target shareworthiness,
  2. large on-target TAM,
  3. problem-solution clarity,
  4. a tactical, non-obvious, immediately implementable fix. If it can't be applied, it's "entertainment in disguise".

## 8. MONETIZATION / CONTENT FOR BUSINESSES

### 8.1 Attention Conversion Funnel (4 multiplicative steps; any zero makes the result zero) [V] (pSIY9ngEZC8)
1. **Bullseye Signaling.**
   - Viewers bucket content as potential solution, entertainment, or irrelevant. Only the solution bucket buys.
   - Tactics:
     - specific topics tied to pains your offer solves,
     - unmistakably clear first 1 or 2 lines (topic, pain, teased solution),
     - name the emotion of not solving it,
     - show the before/after transformation,
     - power words or term branding so the solution feels novel.
2. **Trust Bank / Trust Ladder** (8 rungs, weakest to strongest):

| Rung | Trust signal |
|---|---|
| 8 | Third-party stat or study |
| 7 | Cold testimonial (one stranger) |
| 6 | Borrowed verified proof (a famous person's framework) |
| 5 | Personal results |
| 4 | Group / warm testimonials (bonus for named people) |
| 3 | Demonstration of ability in the content itself |
| 2 | Proof of audience (a big, real following) |
| 1 | Warm endorsement from someone the viewer knows and respects |

   - Rungs stack within a video. He hits rungs 2, 3 and 5 in every intro.
   - Higher pain means less trust is needed.
3. **Clear Ramp.** One off-ramp per video:
   - **lead magnet** (his favorite; captures email),
   - **sales page** (low ticket, or high trust),
   - **call / DM** (high ticket).
   - **Align the ramp tightly with the video's topic** (his analogy: an off-ramp to New York after an hour of talking about New York).
4. **Aligned Offer.**
   - The buyer's question is how likely they are to get the result for this price. Two levers: price and trust.
   - The **"flip point"** is where risk outweighs reward. Price below it, or above it with a guarantee.
   - His **10x rule**: the buyer should get 10x return within 30 days.

### 8.2 Content Minutes [V] (fk6LExgMPxI), [V2] (Castmagic, Creator Science)
- People must consume N minutes of your content before acting:
  - a follow takes about 4 to 5 minutes in most niches,
  - stranger to superfan takes about 90 minutes (roughly 270 twenty-second shorts vs two 1-hour podcasts),
  - each price rung needs more minutes, e.g. a $47 product about 20 minutes, the next tier about 67, high ticket hundreds.
- Minutes only count if the viewer trusts you **and** has the problem.

### 8.3 Modern content stack + 3 metrics [V2] (Creator Science)
- **Stack:** a triangle where short-form and long-form both feed **email ("the golden goose")**.
  - One short-form platform (Instagram or LinkedIn by audience) plus YouTube for long-form.
  - He suggests cracking long-form first, then expanding to short-form for reach.
  - Note that 3y-WiiUaqb4 says the opposite for cash-strapped founders: start with short-form, then fund a YouTube team.
- **3 metrics (ranked):**
  1. **conversions (emails) per video**: every video has a CTA to a lead magnet, tracked with ManyChat,
  2. **followers gained per video**: a follow is worth about 5 to 10 views,
  3. views.
- Benchmark against educational creators, not entertainers.
- Lead magnets: carve pieces of the paid academy into free lead magnets. Pick the topic first, then attach the closest magnet.

### 8.4 Audience of Buyers road map [V, partial read] (oRYfJ_yxz6M)
1. **Define the buyer**: demographics, psychographics, background.
2. **Buyer journey mapping**: their end-to-end workflow today. Blockers (hard, slow, expensive) become topics.
3. **Pain-point baseline identification**: pick one max-pain step and learn what they do today.
4. **New solve**: your non-obvious, implementable alternative. The gap between the old and new solve drives desire to buy. He calls this step where "all the sauce is".
5. **Content packaging**: thumbnail, title and intro; or hook and caption.
- Offer options include going deeper on one workflow step, or a done-for-you / speed-up product.

## 9. PERSONAL BRAND (founder use case)

### 9.1 Personal Brand Positioning Canvas [V] (S-7VkwSU0gs)
Spend the first 2 to 3 days of the 90 on this.
1. **Objective / game.** Mass awareness (mainstream) or targeted awareness with efficient conversion (category famous). It's easier to start narrow and expand than the reverse.
2. **Target audience.** Write an **authority statement**: "I help [X] with [Y] in [Z way]". Use it as an **avatar filter** to prevent **"viewer drift"**, the slide toward vanity topics.
3. **Four personal brand pillars:**
   - **Niche targeting bullseye.** Concentric rings from narrowest outward. The narrower, the faster you own a lane. Expand as proof grows.
   - **Contrarian beliefs.** Keep a **contrarian take vault**: log every time you violently disagree with someone. Aim for 3 to 5 macro contrarian frames with smaller takes under each.
   - **Association & common objects.** Pick the words you want to own in people's memory, repeat them, and use consistent objects (his: hat, black hoodie, the same set, round sunglasses).
   - **Points of uniqueness** on 5 axes: topic, substance/depth, avatar, format, visuals. Map 5 to 10 competitors and find the white space.

### 9.2 Content Formulas + 90-day test plan [V] (S-7VkwSU0gs)
- **Content formula = topic + format + visual layout.** Example: barbecue technique, as a live tutorial, shot standing at the grill.
- Target 3 or 4 topics, 1 to 3 formats and 1 to 3 layouts, giving **5 to 10 formulas**.
- **Phase 1, test (days 1 to 60):**
  - 3 videos in week 1, 5 in week 2, then **7 a week for 6 weeks**, about 50 videos.
  - Test widely. The only filter is whether it gives value to the ideal viewer.
- **Phase 2, scale:**
  - Double down and cut using 4 tests: does it perform on the chosen metric, does it attract the ideal viewer, do we enjoy making it, are we proud of how it looks.
  - The first sprint is 6 months. Formulas burn out, so cycle back into testing.

### 9.3 Cult-like brand [V2] (auPtvaoibgk)
- Cult fans behave differently: they watch all your content (not some), share it (not just watch), and buy (not just think about it).
- Aim for **category fandom** rather than peak fandom.
- **4 content shifts (weights):**
  - **Direction 30%**: useful, non-obvious, "value density".
  - **Comprehension 40%**: bite-sized and simple.
  - **Effectiveness 20%**: the tips actually work.
  - **Likability 10%**: look, confidence, authenticity.
- Timeline: 12 to 24 months.

### 9.4 Speedrun playbook (6 steps) [V, partial] (fk6LExgMPxI)
1. **Series / bingeability.**
   - Hard series: numbered, e.g. "30 lessons before 30".
   - Soft series: a repeated format or opening line.
   - Frame it around a pain, a desire loop or a story arc. One view becomes about 8 views, which clears the follow threshold.
2. **Signature visual style** = look + set + shot framing + editing (motion graphics, type, color).
3. **Unique edge**: new information **or** new delivery.
4. **Hooks**: aim for two dopamine hits before the bounce (visual first, then the words).
5. **Storytelling sequencing.**
6. **Posting & mindset.**
   - 1 video a day at the same time (for accountability, not the algorithm).
   - First 4 or 5 videos warm up the algorithm.
   - Then batches of 10 on the hero series, made one at a time while iterating.
   - Rank videos by followers gained (IG: Professional Dashboard > Content > sort by follows).
   - After 3 batches with no breakout, the cause is usually substance or hooks.
   - The **"igniter"** is the first viral breakout, typically after 5 to 10 good videos. A **"super share"** from a big account is worth about 1,000 normal shares.

## 11. LONG-FORM / YOUTUBE (0 to 100K in 5 months) [V] (3y-WiiUaqb4)
Nine lessons:
1. **Audience matching.** Start with one person, not a topic. Viewers watch for themselves, not for you. The same video got about 1M views on the matched channel and 1,700 on a mismatched one.
2. **Don't post YouTube links on other socials.** It hurts CTR credit and AVD. Email is fine because it is slower-paced than YouTube. If you must promote elsewhere, post a screenshot and tell people to search.
3. **Social credibility line.** Around 10K subscribers on YouTube. Before it, grind 100 to 200 subscribers at a time, or get there through a **"quality shock"** video.
4. **Packaging game.** Title and thumbnail before production; 3-item thumbnail; face emotion matches the title.
5. **Click confirmation** in the first 10 to 20 seconds. Common belief vs contrarian take creates contrast.
6. **Differentiation.** Aim for the best video ever made on the topic (value density). Differentiate on uniqueness (what), distillation (how you say it), or explanation (examples and metaphors). Have proof.
7. **Hire a specialist team.** YouTube takes about 40 hours a week.
8. **Perception of quality**: audio, video, studio.
9. **Expectations.**
   - Plan for **50 videos**: 1 a week for 12 months with zero traction expected. About 10K subscribers at month 12 is typical.
   - AdSense is negligible; he reports about $3K a month on 400K monthly views.
   - The "artist gap" (skill vs taste) closes with reps.
- Summary steps:
  - pick an avatar you can serve for 50 videos,
  - post weekly without missing,
  - make one incremental improvement per video,
  - packaging and ideas first,
  - assume each video costs $1,000 and ask whether it is worth that,
  - plan for zero outliers and reverse-engineer any you get.

## 14. PERFORMANCE ANALYSIS & ITERATION
- **Outlier score** (views ÷ channel average). He graduates a format at **10x or more** (o_n5ED5fE1I) and filters at 5x or more for research.
- **Choose the KPI by game**: views or followers for fandom games; **$ per view or conversions per view** for business games. (o_n5ED5fE1I [V])
- His metric ranking: emails per video, then followers per video, then views. (Creator Science [V2])
- **Short-form diagnostic** when nothing breaks out: check series/idea filtering, signature visual, unique edge, hooks, storytelling. Substance or hooks are usually the cause. (fk6LExgMPxI [V])
- **Packaging diagnostic**: does it get noticed in the grid (color)? Does the title make sense (clarity)? Does the thumbnail confirm it? (jZCupmAOMAE [V])
- **Retention diagnostic**: a steadily decaying graph means missing loop openers or re-hooks. (pcnrzBwoVUk [V])

## 16. PRE-POST CHECKLISTS (for the app)

1. **Idea gate.**
   - Passes the avatar filter?
   - Validated by an outlier?
   - Non-obvious contrarian angle?
   - Key visual exists?
   - Outline points unique?
   - "Would I spend $1,000 on this rep?"
2. **Hook gate.**
   - 4 commandments (alignment, speed to value, clarity, curiosity).
   - One subject and one question.
   - The 4 S's.
   - Power-word slots filled (subject, action, objective, contrast; proof and time optional).
   - Text hook: 2 lines max, about 3 seconds on screen, no connectors, not covering the face.
   - Visual, Audio, Visual sandwich makes sense with the sound off.
3. **Lock-in Zone gate.** The hook claim is confirmed within 5 to 10 seconds; a trust anchor or proof is shown.
4. **Script gate.**
   - Interesting? Compressed? Does the hook work alone? What emotion at the end?
   - Uses but/therefore, not "and then".
   - Jagged sentence lengths.
   - Loop openers every 20 to 30 seconds (60 to 90 seconds in long-form).
   - No hedge words (embedded truths).
   - W's ordered what/why, then who/how, then when/where.
   - Atomic sharable line.
   - Last line written.
5. **Dopamine ladder check.** Stimulation, captivation, anticipation, validation; affection and revelation signals.
6. **Conversion gate.**
   - Bullseye signal in lines 1 and 2.
   - At least 2 trust-ladder rungs.
   - A CTA ramp aligned with the topic.
   - Offer below the flip point.
7. **Packaging gate.**
   - YouTube: title locked, thumbnail at most 3 elements, works in dark mode, inverts the niche color meta, 1/16-size legible, bottom-right clear, intro confirms the click within about 4 seconds.
   - Short-form: custom cover, first caption line written as a curiosity title.
