## 0. Master principle: pick your game, then work backwards (00:58)
- "The environment is bifurcating." There aren't one or two strategies any more; "there's like hundreds now. You can win with any, but you have to pick and align the right strategy."
- **The 5 Content Games** (named in episode 1, recapped here):
  1. **Mainstream fame**
  2. **Category fandom**: fame inside one category, monetised by brand deals. His @kallaway tech account is this game.
  3. **Selling a commodity product**: the buyer doesn't need to trust the person (e.g. Bloom soda; Greg LaVecchia).
  4. **Selling an explanatory product**
  5. **Accruing authority**: a personal-brand play where people must trust *you* to buy.
- Questions to answer before any content:
  - What is your intention or goal?
  - What is your offer?
  - Which game are you playing?
  - Does it matter if authority accrues to *you*?
  - Face or faceless?
- "Get very clear on your end goal and work backwards on the strategy." (02:23)
- **The media game alone ≠ empire:** "If you're trying to build an empire you can't play just the media game. You have to have your own products, your own services." (02:05)
- **View-maxing is done:** "V-maxing is f***ing done… views are cooked." (1:37) Don't start a crowded brand-deal channel today. Category fandom still works only in *untapped* categories, e.g. "become the robotics guy."

## 1. Why content stopped working: the "slop layer" (02:42–03:55)
- Platforms are cracking down on AI content: YouTube and Meta give an "immediate ban, immediate throttle" when they detect it. That surprised him.
- The bigger problem is **"human slop"**: real people copying other creators' scripts **word for word** in every niche. Together with AI slop this forms "a slop layer at the bottom" that commoditises generic formats.

## 2. FRAMEWORK: The Format Pyramid (04:14–10:28)
Four levels, bottom to top. Moving up is harder to make and reaches a smaller audience, but is more defensible.

| Level | Name | What it is | Example formats |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (base) | **Facts** | Listing or ranking with no opinion. "Least talent lives at the bottom." You could ask ChatGPT for a list of 10 and read it out. | lists, rankings 1–5 |
| 2 | **Explanation** | Facts **+ your opinion/insight** | breakdowns, tutorials, how-to (most of his Kallaway Marketing content) |
| 3 | **Application** | Showing or proving how to **apply** it to get a result. "You can't fake that shit." | case studies, scenarios, hypotheticals: *"If I had to grow a plastic surgeon from zero to a million followers, here's how I would do it."* |
| 4 (top) | **Stories** | One-of-one personal experiences from your job or life | first-person stories |

- **The red line rule:** As a niche saturates, a red line rises from the base. "If you're below the line you're f***ed": too many people make those commoditised formats. Saturation first eats Facts, then Explanation, leaving only Application and Stories.
- **Rules:**
  - Open niche (few creators): any level works.
  - Crowded niche: **skip Facts entirely. Start with Explanation + Application. Go "straight to application content"** so viewers think "this person actually knows what they're talking about" after one video.
  - Stories are hard to ramp from zero.
- His read on the social-media-growth niche: tier 1 "completely cooked", tier 2 "almost fully cooked". He's moving up to application and story formats.
- **Decide formats from your proof:** "Have you actually done this, or are you a beginner accelerating?" If you've done it, lead with what you've done, e.g. a surgeon showing real cases versus a med student.
- Mark's addition: Stories have a **1A** tier (your own experiences, "one of one content", the final moat) and a **1B** tier (third-party stories such as Steve Jobs or Elon).
- Kallaway's version of the final moat: "I did this. This is how I did it. I can do it for you." (10:20)

## 3. FRAMEWORK: The 4 Ways Left to Stay Competitive (10:29–29:45)
**Better ideas → more advanced → better proof → better storytelling.**

### 3.1 Better Ideas: "the 800-pound gorilla" (10:44)
- "The 80/20, 90/10, 95/5 of content is ideas." Edits, formats and hooks matter, but ideas matter by far the most.
- **Idea = Topic + Angle.**
  - **Topic** has 2 levels: the *topic bucket* and the *specific topic*.
  - **Angle** = "my unique perspective on that."
- **Topic buckets follow Pareto.** In every niche a few buckets always outperform, because "the average viewer has the same psychology as the other average viewer." Viewers rank problems by what they *think* their problems are.
  - Example (social-media-growth niche): **storytelling, hooks, AI workflows, strategy** always win. **Ideas, recording, delivery** always lose, even with "the craziest sauce on recording."
- **Procedure:**
  1. Find the topic buckets viewers prioritise.
  2. Inside them, ask "what do I know that's different from everyone else?" That is your **contrarian take**.
- **Topic TAM × shock score of your take.** You need both. A big-TAM topic (American football rather than badminton) gets you drowned out without a unique take.
  - "Some non-obvious truth that you know to be true… ideally also in the big topic TAM."
- **Explore vs exploit:**
  - Explorer: find topic buckets no one covers. "99% of the time you'll be wrong, but the 1% will be huge."
  - Exploiter: find the winners and double down "for the first six months, then you'll earn the right to experiment."
  - **Recommendation for business owners who aren't full-time creators: exploit (the 99%).** Especially on a deadline ("get this objective in 100 days").
  - Mark adds: copycats are so fast now that exploring for the 1% isn't worth it.

### 3.2 FRAMEWORK: DCT (15:09)
- **D = Data → topics.** "Let the data tell me the topics."
- **C = Creativity → angle.** "I come up with some crazy shit about a topic that's validated."
- **T = Transpositioning → formats.** If formats in your niche are saturated, borrow one from *outside* the niche. Example: a top-down shot from a vintage hat-making creator.
- Related: **"Data-enabled creativity"** (49:38). "The goal is not to copy… I want to learn what's working with the data so that I can be creative in the areas that make the most sense."

### 3.3 More Advanced: sophistication of the angle (15:50)
- "The way to print money is high-ticket, high-margin offers sold to rich customers." Rich customers are sophisticated, at "a level 3, 4, 5 out of 10."
- **Rule:** Skip level 1–2 beginner material and pitch at **4–6/10**. Target viewer: a business owner at **3–8/10** content understanding (2:03:45).
- Trade-off: views fall because the TAM shrinks, but conversion rises and the ICP becomes more valuable. "I don't want beginners that are broke… I only want people… willing to spend five grand right now because this guy thinks in my mode."

### 3.4 Turn the view dial down on purpose (17:08–20:40)
- "Most people actually want 10,000 views, 5,000 views, but extremely dialed… high-value customer." "Check the ego."
- His numbers:
  - Kallaway Marketing now gets about **10K views/video** (it did 400K while he was proving he could grow it). No video over 1M. "Designed only for conversion."
  - @kallaway tech goes for home runs every video (1M on an Elon video).
- "If you post narrow, it'll find the people you need. You just have to do it for a while." Organic targeting is as precise as Meta paid-ads targeting: same algorithms.
- Playing the view game means playing the CPM game. "Usually people have to play the view game for a little bit to realise the conversion is not there."
- Result of going narrow: high-value CEOs in his DMs saying "I'll pay you anything… one on one."

### 3.5 Better Proof: "the ultimate separator" (20:55–28:20)
- Hormozi: "The proof isn't in the pudding, it *is* the pudding."
- Commodity products don't need personal proof. **Authority plays do.**
- **Purchase = a risk game:** "I'm going to risk X dollars to get 2X, 3X back. If you've done that many times with people just like me, I buy easier. Proof de-risks the purchase decision."
- **No proof → demonstrate ability** so well that people think "they really sound like they know what they're talking about." See Trust Ladder, §6.
- **One-of-one proof:** claims only you can make, e.g. "#1 on Amazon" for Greg's drink, or Hormozi's Guinness record for most non-fiction sales in 24 hours.
  - Being #1 in a category is "way, way better" than #2.
  - **Accumulate proof moments**, even granular ones ("#1 in X category in one store").
  - "If you have proof and you're burying it, you're doing yourself a disservice."
- **Proof audit procedure:**
  1. Canvass the niche: who serves the customer today, and what proof do they have?
  2. Can I beat their proof? If yes, go get it.
  3. If not, reposition into a **sub-lane where nobody has proof above me.**
  - Proof must be "differentiated relative to the bar in your niche." His own proof bar: 2M followers, billions of views, 3 seven-figure businesses in 3 years. Compete with that on the same track without better proof and "you lose."
- **"Infinity stones of business models":** Hormozi has gym launch (services), supplements (ecom), ALAN (software), media, and consulting, so he has proof for every buyer type.
  - Kallaway copies this: media + info/coaching + software, plus two media plays (tech = brand deals, marketing = conversion). "Build up the portfolio of proof across every nook and cranny."
- **Mark's "flexing" lesson** (22:28):
  - He used to hide IKONICK's numbers. Now he does "minimal subtle flexing", e.g. "the first $6M in revenue I was customer service."
  - **Zoom-out practice:** when a post shapes how you see someone, pause, close the app, and ask "how can I influence others the same way?" Then show your own numbers.
  - "Don't be too salesy, never talk about your numbers. I'm done with that bullshit."

### 3.6 Better Storytelling (28:28)
- "Story is just a way of sequencing words to trigger emotion." Facts delivered "at a machine-gun cadence" carry almost zero emotion, and **emotion drives purchase**.
- **First-person POV story template:** **"I had this problem/complication → I took this action → I got this result."** Viewers see themselves in you and the emotion transfers.
- "Your goal is to go from facts to stories and add more emotional resonance as you go."

## 4. Distribution is the final moat; rented → owned (30:03–34:40)
- **Business reduced to 3 atomic elements:**
  1. **Traffic/attention** (distribution)
  2. **Funnels** (where you put the attention; can be as simple as video → button → product)
  3. **Offer/product**
- AI is shrinking the moat around product (anyone can vibe-code), and Sandcastles-type tools are shrinking the distribution moat. Both moats exist today; "in five years both are zero." Window: about 3–5 years (3 on the front end, 5–6 at the long end). **Take advantage now.**
- **Rented platforms:** any feed-based platform (X, IG, YouTube). They can ban you and your reach goes to zero, and they will get noisy with AI content.
- **Owned channels:** email list (exportable from beehiiv), private community.
- "**Ramp people from rented to owned as fast as you can.** Priority number one right now." Build "systems and infrastructure that drive as many people from the rented platform to my own email list or communities, as fast and as big as you can."
- **Modern content stack (1:54):** **short form + long form + email.** Each pipes into email *separately*; cross-promoting short↔long is "not the best."
  - Short form = broad, eager viewers and **way more email sign-ups**, because you can't deliver as much value inside the video.
  - Long form = "the GOAT."

## 5. FRAMEWORK: Short-form lead-magnet engine, 10–15K net emails/month (34:57–39:37)
Steps:
1. **Post 1×/day** on the conversion channel: deep, advanced material "the type of person I want wants."
2. **Make one lead magnet per video, tightly aligned to that video's pain point.** "If you just say 'give me your email,' no one would do it."
3. **Aggravate a pain point in the video → offer the solution via the lead magnet.** "Cut them, give them the band-aid." Lead-magnet formats: PDF, guide, Claude skill, video.
   - Congruent: aggravate "you're bad at storytelling" → offer "**the 8 principles to make your stories better**."
   - Incongruent: aggravate storytelling → offer "here's my editors."
4. Delivery through a **ManyChat comment keyword** ("comment STORY") → collect email → send the doc.
5. **Nurture.** "Just because you have their email doesn't mean you own them." Keep sending value without bothering them. Over time you chip away at their doubt that you can deliver, or wait until they have the problem.
6. **Aligned funnel** (his Q4 priority): **content → lead magnet → landing page → low-ticket product → high-ticket program, all on the SAME problem** (e.g. storytelling video → storytelling LM → storytelling low-ticket → storytelling-driven high-ticket page). "The problem doesn't change… that's what the real pros are doing."

Lead-magnet rules:
- **Less is more.** "I don't want more work, I want the answer." Don't brag about a 10-page PDF; promise "one click → the answer."
- **Name it descriptively, with power words, like copywriting.**
- **Narrow, per-video LMs beat broad multi-topic LMs.** You learn each subscriber's exact pain point and can cross-sell adjacent topics.
- **Paid-ads analogy:** "Instead of a paid ad you have a free video; instead of a landing page you have a lead magnet." Keep the messaging angle aligned all the way through.

## 6. FRAMEWORK: The Trust Ladder (39:57–41:55)
- There are "eight levels". He only gave the simplified version on air.
- **Top (#1): warm endorsement** from someone you already trust ("you co-sign me → they pay immediately").
- **#2–3: proof of result.** "I've done the exact thing you want, with someone just like you."
- **Lower: demonstration of ability through non-obvious insight.**
  - Aim for **"light bulb moments" / "dopamine hits"**: "oh wow, I never thought of that." Target **about 2 per video, across several videos in a row**. Enough of them can equal the trust that proof gives.
  - "Demonstration of ability is worse than proof, but it can still get trust if you do it enough."
- **Why copying fails:** "If I've heard something before and then hear it from you, zero additional trust goes to you." "Trust comes from hearing novel things over and over."
- **Goldilocks insight:** "Not obvious, but as soon as they hear it, it sounds obvious."

## 7. Skill before content (41:57–44:02)
- "The way to make money with content is to have expert-level skills. **Content is just a vehicle to amplify attention on those skills and monetize around it.**"
- **Gut check:** "Do I have top 10%, ideally top 1%, skills in my area?" If yes, "you're just content frameworks and a workflow away from printing." If no, obsess over one skill first (paid ads, copywriting, sales, product…).
- Reason: "You don't have non-obvious takes if you haven't done the thing beyond where most people have gone."
- His origin: **18 months of only short form, one video every day, about 8 hours per video**, until he was top 1%. Then he made content about how he learned it.

## 8. Research & review workflow: "data-enabled creativity" (Sandcastles + Claude demo, 45:29–1:12:10)
Tool-agnostic logic is given first; his Sandcastles specifics follow in parentheses.

**Vision:** "Take my content brain and put it into software." Answer a few questions, get scripts in your voice with your takes. This compresses about **4 hours/video** of research and writing down to just recording and editing. "If you're a business owner, you **record 10 in one hour**, send them off to the editing team." ### Step 1: Build a competitor watch list (50:00)
- Pick creators who are (a) doing well in your space, (b) "talking about the stuff I want to talk about", and (c) "talking to the people I want to attract." About 10 is enough.
- Discover them via niche keywords, e.g. "social media growth strategies", Instagram only, **10K–1M followers**.
- Find look-alikes of a favourite creator (double-arrow "closest proximity" sort).

### Step 2: Pull outlier videos (51:35)
- **Filters:** posted in the **last 3 months** (or 3–6), **engagement > 2%** (screens out paid brand deals that inflate views). **Sort by Outlier Score.**
- **Outlier Score = video views ÷ that creator's rolling 3-month average before the video was posted.**
  - Example: 710K views on a ~10K-average channel = 75.6×. That's an apples-to-apples metric across channel sizes.
  - **Study small-creator outliers disproportionately.** They don't have face credibility, "so it's the idea that actually worked."
  - For old videos on a channel that used to be tiny, set an **outlier ceiling (e.g. 200×)** and/or limit to the last 12 months.
- The result "at the simplest level is a hit list of the best-performing topics in your niche."

### Step 3: Deep-analyse each top video (about 60 s each; do the top 50–100) (53:13)
Fields to extract:
- full transcript
- story sections
- **topic**
- **unique angle / contrarian take**
- core facts
- **hook**: exact formula, word-for-word text, and why it worked
- **format**
- category
- **visual layout**

### Step 4: Batch-analyse in Claude (CSV/JSON export or MCP) (53:59)
- "This alone is not that valuable on one video. What's valuable is when you take the top 50 or top 100 with all this data… run analysis in batch → non-obvious insights."
- Example questions:
  - What topics are working and what should I make more of?
  - Which hooks?
  - "What power phrases are hitting that I'm missing?"
  - Which formats?
  - "What's working for this guy that I'm not doing?"
  - "Compare our two channels."
- **Pro tip:** end prompts with **"build in dynamic HTML"** to get a visual interactive report.
- Give Claude a **context layer** first: upload your scripts, save your goals to memory, or ask it to "interview me about what I care about, save all this context." Then it can pitch ideas proactively.
  - "It's really hard for it to pitch you ideas unless it knows what's worked for you."

### Step 5: Weekly auto-report (55:24)
- A dashboard report runs **every 7 days** on best-performing **topics, hooks, formats, and script-writing tactics**.
- Hooks are codified into buckets (about "20 types of hooks", e.g. "tutorial hooks").
- Example script tactics:
  - Worked: "tool tutorial script with numbered steps".
  - Didn't work: "live demonstration of the technique mid-script".
- **Reference library:** 105 collections = top **22 formats** (e.g. About Me, Breakdown, Case Study…), editing styles, signature series, visual hooks.

### Own-channel strategy audit: run every ~2 weeks (1:00:26–1:05:55)
- **His prompt (paraphrased closely):** "Pull in all the videos from [channel] (already deep-analysed). Build a super visual report breaking down the best-performing topics, hooks, formats, script-writing tactics and any non-obvious patterns I might be missing. Give me the full breakdown of all videos organised however is most important, then tell me exactly what I should be doing on my next batch. Build in dynamic HTML."
- Run it on the **whole channel**, not just the last 49.
- What it found when his views dropped in August:
  - **"Four straight months with a monster, then August."** Every month had at least one breakout (psychology breakdowns of storytelling); August had none.
  - **Overcooked an asset:** he re-ran a winning "turn Claude into a machine" asset until it stopped working.
  - **CTA fatigue:** too many CTAs to Sandcastles in August. "Back off, drive CTAs to other stuff."
  - Topic-bucket share of views: AI/Sandcastles workflows > storytelling > hooks > strategy (weak).
  - **"Anytime you name the mechanism, it works super well."** Naming his own framework lifts performance.
- Follow-up prompts:
  - "In my next batch of **seven**, what should I do differently?"
  - "Give me topics based on everything you know about me." Example output: streak psychology, story arc, "5 desire hook templates".
- **Cadence:** "at minimum once every couple of weeks." Set a **recurring scheduled task in Claude every 2 weeks that pings you in Slack.**

### Niche analysis, for new creators: run every 3–4 weeks (1:05:57–1:09:02)
1. Take the top 100 analysed niche videos and **screen out off-topic ones** (life updates etc.; 15 removed, 85 left).
2. Get topic performance (e.g. "script writing & storytelling" best, then craft/resources, hooks, content strategy, AI content), plus formats, hook formulas and **script structures** (e.g. "the levels ladder").
3. Ask follow-ups: "Give me an actual example from the transcript… write me a levels-ladder script with my context."
4. **Leaderboard → idea test.** Open each top video in order and ask **"Do I have something unique to say about this or not?"** If no, skip; if yes, "there's a good video that's been validated."
   - Example: Sky10's "best short-form script structure" video → Kallaway made his own version with his own take. "Only thing that's the same is he made a video about the script-writing structure… It's just aiming my creativity."
- Cadence: "once a month, once every three weeks", because not every new post is an outlier.

### Hook analysis on your own top ~139 hooks (CSV) (1:09:03–1:11:35)
- **Winning hook psychology (his data):**
  - **transformation object** (take one thing to another)
  - **impossibility clause** ("impossible to skip / ignore")
  - **addiction** language
  - **shock that a new thing exists**

  - **fear/mistake framing** ("this will never work for you if…") → "lean to optimism"
  - **multi-sentence hooks**
- Diagnostic prompt: "In the last two weeks I made 14 videos, only 2 worked. What did I do wrong on the hooks for the other 12?"
- **Hook generator:** feed a topic → 4 hooks modelled on your best hooks → "riff ×4" variations. Examples generated for "the 3-2-1 script structure":
  - "Here's how to turn a blank page into a storytelling machine."
  - "Here's how to make your scripts so structured it's scientifically impossible to skip."
  - "Here's how to get people addicted to your structure before they realise what hit them."
  - With no videos of your own, seed it with niche hooks.
- **His personal hook-writing order (1:52:31): written hook → data → tweak** (not data → hook). He writes down what comes to mind first, as when he wrote rap lyrics over a beat, then iterates. "If you just go on the data and copy everything it becomes very clony."

## 9. FRAMEWORK: The Growth System, a zero-to-plan for a new expert (plastic-surgeon demo, 1:12:16–1:27:00)
"People pay like 10 grand to a strategist for weeks of work and we just did this in 30 minutes." The expert only answers the questions and approves.

3. **Her dream outcome?**
4. **Her biggest pain points?** "Extremely important." Use what you've heard in client conversations.
5. **Your unique differentiation / unfair advantage?** Example: "I only do faces; a third of my practice is revisions of other surgeons' facelifts."
6. (One more question about current activity was implied but not read aloud.)

**Step 8: FRAMEWORK: Format Matrix.**

## 16. [I] Synthesis: idea → publish → review workflow for the app (built only from the episode's frameworks)
**A. Setup (one-off, refresh quarterly)**
1. Pick the game: one of the 5 Content Games; for a founder, usually *authority* + *explanatory product*. Define the offer and the goal metric (views, followers or conversions).
2. Run the Skill gut-check (top 10% / 1%?) and list proof assets. Do the proof audit against competitors: beat them or pick a sub-lane.
3. Niche Interviewer: offer & buyer, Frankenstein ICP, dream outcome, pain points, unfair advantage.
4. Build a watch list of about 10 competitors (8 Tier-A + 3 adjacent).
5. Fill the Positioning Wheel (7 slices → white space).
6. Draw the Audience Bullseye (5 rings; dark blue is banned).
7. Choose the 3-2-1 buckets: 1 broad, 2 narrow, plus a chaos-agent slot.

**B. Weekly loop (a batch of 7)**
1. Pull outliers from the last 3 months (>2% engagement, sorted by outlier score = views ÷ rolling 3-month average).
2. Map them to buckets.
3. Apply the **"Do I have a non-obvious take?"** filter.
4. Fill 7 slots: 2 broad + 4 narrow + 1 chaos agent. Tag each with a Format Pyramid level (aim for Explanation/Application/Story; avoid Facts in crowded niches) and a format (storytelling structure × visual layout).
5. For each idea:
   - **topic + angle** (contrarian take, shock score)
   - **proof line** (one-of-one if possible)
   - **story beat** (problem → action → result)
   - **≥2 light-bulb moments**
   - **name the mechanism**
6. Hook: write first, then check against data and tweak. Use winning patterns (transformation number/object, impossibility, addiction, "new thing exists", famous name, second-person confrontation, viewer as subject, declarative named laws). Avoid "If you…", fear framing and multi-sentence hooks.
7. Script: cut middle fluff, speed to value, keep it as short as possible.
8. Attach a **pain-aligned lead magnet + ManyChat keyword** to every video. Short and descriptive.
9. Batch-record (10 in 1 hr) → editor → publish 1×/day.

**C. Review**
- Weekly report on topics, hooks, formats and script tactics.
- **Every 2 weeks:** own-channel audit. Check for monthly "monster" presence, overcooked assets, CTA fatigue, and the next-7 recommendations.
- **Every 3–4 weeks:** niche scan.
- Track only views, followers and conversions. Diagnose flops in the order topic → angle → hook.
- Funnel: LM → low-ticket → high-ticket, all on the same problem. Nurture the email list with value.
