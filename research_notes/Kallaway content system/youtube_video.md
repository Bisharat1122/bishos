## 0. The core model (2:23–4:07)

- **"YouTube is a click-and-watch platform."** On TikTok and Reels you are *served* content. On long-form YouTube the viewer still *chooses* what to click.
- **The algorithm values three things:** (1) people clicking, (2) people watching, (3) people being satisfied.
- **The game has four parts:**
  1. Better ideas than anyone else (ideas that "travel").
  2. Make them clickable. **"The click part of the equation is kind of 50% of the game."**
  3. Keep people watching as long as possible, and leave them satisfied.
  4. Do it again: build a repeatable, momentum-building channel.
- **"Virality can be engineered… a lot more predictable than people think"** (1:43).
- **Brand failure mode:** "Vimeo creators". Brands treat YouTube as a place to host videos instead of applying YouTube strategy (1:11).
- **Red Bull proof point:** 400–500K views per video when he started in 2022, now 8–9M (10–20x).

## 1. Three Circles: market, idea ceiling, execution (+ the floor) (9:25–13:47)

There are three nested circles to assess together:

1. **All viewers you could reach.** This is set by the niche/market (TAM).
   - Big TAMs are more competitive but have more upside.
   - Small niches build momentum faster but hit a ceiling.
2. **The ceiling your *idea* imposes.** "Your actual idea itself can just set a ceiling." Ask: *"Why did you think most people in your niche would watch this?"*
3. **Execution.** It decides whether the idea travels as far as it *should*.

**The floor** is the views you would get from a "trash video". His test: *"if we literally just posted a video titled 'video one' and a picture of our face, how many viewers would that get?"* That number is your most dedicated core. Everything above it is execution plus idea (13:07).

**The job when entering a channel:** (a) make the market bigger, (b) find ideas that reach further into it, (c) execute well enough.

**Examples of widening a niche idea:**
- **Astrophotography.** Telescope reviews and tutorials got 1–2K views.
  - The new video "shooting the Milky Way with iPhone vs expensive camera / 10 min vs 1 h vs 100 h" got 2–3M views.
  - It borrowed the viral "10-second vs 10-minute vs 10-hour haircut" format (11:33).
- **"I built a wildlife pond in my backyard"** got ~25–30M views. A how-to on pond lining would get ~10K (12:05).
- **"No niche is too small"** as long as there is some interest: knitting, horticulture, and golf ("people said golf doesn't work… it's one of the biggest niches now").
  - A small niche can still be high-value. A Wix-vs-Squarespace reviewer with 100K viewers has very valuable viewers.

## 2. Niche and positioning: Skill × Demand, small-channel outliers, start narrow (15:53–21:00, 29:17–33:24)

- **Two circles:** (your passions, skills, competitive advantages) ∩ (what is in demand on YouTube now, or will be). "That intersection is usually where I like to live."
  - Don't chase hot niches (Minecraft, Roblox, the latest business fad) just because they go viral.
- **Evidence of demand = small channels that overperform.** "I learn a lot from this tiny channel with 100 subscribers that just posted a video that got 3,000 views."
  - VC analogy: a tiny company growing from 10K to 30K/month. Reverse-engineer whether it is the niche or the execution.
- **Outlier score = video views ÷ channel average views** (for example 30x or 50x).
  - Tools: 1of10, ViewStats, vidIQ. "The tool doesn't matter… so long as it shows the average and the outlier score."
- **Subscribers are "kind of dead… a vanity metric"** and do not matter much to the algorithm.
  - Rough benchmark: typical views per video ≈ **~20% of subscriber count**. Above that means you are overperforming.
  - Paddy: 200–300K subs, 1–2M views per episode (20:03).
- **Topic overlap is much smaller than people think** (29:39):
  - Red Bull's channel mixed parkour, snowboarding, climbing, F1 and soccer. "Very few people in the world… care about all these… and the people that do, they work for Red Bull."
  - Call of Duty ↔ Battlefield overlap is about **20%** (his guess).
  - Bodybuilding ≠ yoga ≠ stretching.
- **Start narrow, expand gradually.** "You can't really start broad and go narrow… You can start narrow and just gradually expand" (32:15).
  - Example: Forrest Galante (now the #1 wildlife channel) was signed in 2023 at 20–30K views per video.
  - He started with a niche within the niche (extinct and dangerous animals, the thylacine), then expanded to zoo tours and big cats with millions of views.
- **Host's version:** e-com operators → CPG operators → business operators broadly.

## 3. CCN framework: Core / Casual / New viewers (21:03–29:00, 33:24–39:00)

"Created ~4–5 years ago… it actually sums up content strategy as a whole." It applies to every platform.

| Group | Definition | Example size (host's podcast) |
|---|---|---|
| **Core** | Ride-or-die, the "10,000 true fans". Watch no matter what. Starting out at zero, you have none. | 3–5K of 100K viewers |
| **Casual** | Dip in and out, watching ~1 in 3 or 1 in 4 videos. Valuable because they can be converted to core. | — |
| **New** | Everyone in your TAM you haven't reached yet. | Business-operator podcast TAM ≈ 500K–1M |

**Rule:** every video should be watchable and enjoyable by core, casual AND new viewers. **"Hit all three."**

**Why it's hard:** the core's favourite topics can repel new viewers, and new-viewer bait can alienate the core.

**Mistake 1: over-serving the core.**
- "If you ask your comment section what they want, they'll just say more of the old thing you used to do that no longer works" (Henry Ford's "faster horse").
- "I don't really listen to my comment section that much."
- Hormozi analogy: the video operators *want* from him would get 10K views.

**Mistake 2: chasing new viewers with no through-line.** "Come over here, grab you with this idea…" leaves no reason to keep watching the channel.

**Mistake 3 (entrepreneurs especially):** assuming more overlap between interests than exists (see §2).

**Worked example: Jesser, three videos in the same month (25:52–28:00):**
- **Core only:** "Official reveal of my new YouTube studio, full tour" got ~400K. That is the core audience size: "an easy way to see what your core audience is."
- **Core + Casual:** "Insane make-the-shot I'll-buy-you-anything challenge" got just under 1M. A familiar format with some stakes but "kind of basic."
- **Core + Casual + New:** "Going to 5 NBA games in 50 hours" got **14M**. Basketball TAM ≈ 10–20M.
  - Why it worked: difficulty, a challenge, and **effort bias** ("if we see someone doing something that feels high effort, we… go 'Oh wow'").

**CCN inside the video (the "Katie" example, 37:16):**
- Don't leave gaps that confuse new viewers, such as kissing a girlfriend nobody was told about.
- Don't re-introduce everything either, because that bores the core.
- **Seed context inside the action instead:** "Katie, look, I know you're my girlfriend, but can you go do this really quickly?"
- Never say "you probably saw my last video" or "as we discussed last week."
- Filter question: *"Can someone who has never watched me before watch this video and enjoy it?"*
- Host's takeaway: **"All roads lead to CCN."** Filter title, thumbnail and content through it.

## 4. Hallmarks of 10M–100M+ view videos (33:24–40:00)

1. **CCN fit** (above).
2. **Understood in one second: the "glance test".**
   - Viewers scroll TV and phone feeds giving each video about 1 second.
   - "Every video I've worked on that's had 10 million views… you can always just say them in one second."
   - **If you can't put the idea in a one-line logline, it is not a good idea**, "just a random thought." "I just need a way to package it" means the idea is bad.
3. **Universal appeal.** Make videos "interesting to the biggest YouTuber in the world and my mother at the same time."
   - Like great kids' movies: layered, so they work for kids and adults.
   - Paddy took YouTube-education content from 5–15K views ("5 packaging tips") to ~1M per episode with this rule.
4. **"Pose a question that you have to click to answer"**, so the viewer feels they must **"click to unpause."**
   - Test: *if someone was about to do something important, would this make them watch first?*
   - The host calls this the curiosity gap.

## 5. Three sources of ideas: Internal / External / Innovation (40:11–49:00)

1. **Internal = what already worked for you.**
   - First move on any new client: find past overperformers and twist, adapt, improve, or *just remake* them.
   - "Content moves fast. People have forgotten about it. Do it again."
   - His Instagram page Discovering Ireland reposted the same 5–6 photos every year.
   - Copy yourself first, because strategy is now common and others will copy your hits fast.
   - Even 5K views on a 1K-average channel counts as an outlier.
2. **External = "outlier ideation".**
   - Find videos in your niche **and adjacent niches** that beat their channel average by **≥3–4x**. Example: a 100K-average channel with a 300–400K video.
   - Window: the last **365 days**. For a brand-new channel, go back about **2 years**, which could be ~1,000 outliers. Then ask "what is my spin/twist on each one?"
   - The cause is usually **packaging (title/thumbnail)**, not editing or the intro.
   - Compare the outlier with the same channel's normal (100K) videos: did they change format, style or title?
   - Adjacent-niche remix: golf ↔ fishing (same demographic, gear-heavy pastime). "Fished with wooden rods" becomes "a round of golf with wooden clubs."
3. **Innovation = genuinely new.** Only about **20% of your videos** for small creators ("unless you're a genius like Casey Neistat").
   - Method: **combine two existing things.** Paddy combined Hollywood video-essay breakdowns with business whiteboard videos to create YouTube-channel video essays (2016).

**Host's sequencing (Paddy agreed):** start **External** (no data yet) → after some videos, **Internal** → at scale, **Innovation** (46:29).

**"Frankenstein" case: Noah Kagan, "I asked millionaires how to make a million dollars" (49:06–52:00):**
- Starting point: a 5–10K views per video channel.
- **Concept** came from Daniel Mac's short-form "asking supercar owners what they do". That is a short-form idea turned into long-form.
- **Title** adapted Hormozi's "I asked a billionaire how to make a billion dollars."
- **Thumbnail** copied a ~9M-view small-channel video, "Asking Canadians to cook them dinner": door answered, a shocked person, a backwards cap, outstretched hands, yellow text in the middle.
- Result: **5M views**, "the most viral business video of 2022". It launched the door-knocking trend in business YouTube.
- Underlying habit: **surround yourself with outliers.** "I can pull videos from my brain that were viral on a channel 6 years ago."

## 6. The 100 → 10 → 1 ideation funnel (Apple / Steve Jobs) (52:31–1:01:45)

Steps in order:

1. **Guiding principles.** Define who the right viewers are for your channel (CCN). "It's not always about total viewers… the right viewers."
2. **Apply the 80% Strategy Rule** (not Pareto).
   - Topics should have **~80% audience overlap** with each other, and you stay in that zone **80% of the time**.
   - The other 20% (1 in every 4–5 videos) is for experiments.
   - Test: *"Can 80% of the people who watch this video watch this video, and this video?"*
3. **Generate ~100 ideas per 1 video**, pulling from internal, external and innovation.
   - Paddy's agency: 4–5 people write **200–500 ideas per client per month** in a spreadsheet, and the client sees **5**.
   - "If we want 5 ideas, we probably have to come up with 500."
4. **Cut to ~10 with elimination criteria.** Use 4–5 yes/no gates, for example:
   - **Packageable?** ("Can we easily title/thumbnail it?") This one gate alone kills about **2/3** of ideas. "How do we put this in a thumbnail? … We're getting rid of the idea."
   - **Feasible?** ("Can't put a car on the moon.")
   - **Clears the aspirational view target?** For a 100K-average show, set the target at 150–200K. A guest likely to get 20–30K means "you probably shouldn't do that episode" (if the goal is growth).
   - **Paddy's own channel gate:** he only analysed creators averaging over 1M views, because his target was 1M per video.
   - **Calibration:** if 100 ideas leave 30, "your criteria is garbage." If they leave 1–2, it is too strict.
5. **One-page "idea slide" for each of the 10 finalists:**
   - rough thumbnail sketch
   - rough title(s)
   - rough skeleton/outline
   - logline
   - "Nine of these 10 will never see the light of day."
6. **Pick one.** "If we had to put our career on one of these videos, which one is the best?" Solo version: "gun to the head."

- Host (guest booking): "**It's about packaging first**… if we can't package it… it's not going to travel."
- The host's show also uses a guest-vetting score (distribution, communication skills, ICP fit), a blended average built as a Claude skill.

## 7. Packaging economics: nonlinear returns (1:03:17–1:09:40)

- **"Algorithms are multipliers."** A 20% better title or thumbnail can bring 40x–100x more views. NBA analogy: 10–20% better players earn 200x more.
- **Case (Gabe, web-design channel):** only the thumbnail of an existing video was changed. "**Within a day… went from 1,000 views a day to 40,000 views a day**." The new thumbnail was maybe 20–30% better.
- **Time allocation:** packaging is 50% of results but **not 50% of time**. Spend **10–20% of total production time** on it (10–20 hrs of a 100-hr video).
  - That time covers: researching every title/thumbnail in the niche, multiple designers, studying 80s movie posters, sometimes **50 titles** and **45–50 thumbnails** for big clients.
- **Packaging = idea + title + thumbnail + how much they enjoyed your last video.**
  - Example: Outdoor Boys posts screenshot thumbnails and still wins on brand trust.
- **Changing an established thumbnail style: go incremental.**
  - Host's Peter Rahal / David Protein thumbnail kept 80% the same (guest POV, colour) and added images on the left, and it went "nuclear."
  - Don't throw away recognisability.

## 8. Packaging workflow: "10 titles, 3 thumbnails, every video" (1:14:07–1:20:34)

1. **Idea** (the "gladiator" that survived 500).
2. **Titles first, at least 10.** The thumbnail must *complement* the title, so the title comes first and saves thumbnail work. "I just said five in 5 seconds."
3. **Thumbnail brief** written *before* the shoot:
   - **~5 concepts** to capture on the day, with angles and poses.
   - Use hand sketches, stick figures or AI image generation for reference.
   - Pet peeve: big creators "shoot the video and then afterwards… okay what's the thumbnail?"
4. **Thumbnail shoot:** **10–30 shots per concept** (300–400 photos total) with different angles, facing directions, micro-expressions and lighting.
5. **Design at least 3 fully designed, *differentiated* thumbnails** from the source photos. Changing only the shirt colour does not count.
   - Paddy once made "3 thumbnails per video" a contract line item and turned away clients who refused, even at $100K/month.
   - Outsourcing to a good designer costs "a couple hundred bucks". 1of10 also has a thumbnail generator.
6. **Publish:** put in the one title the team would bet their career on, plus the best 3 thumbnails in **YouTube's native A/B test** ("we use it with every client… across a billion views a month").

**Thumbnail principles from the Red Bull case** (World's Fastest Camera Drone vs F1 Car, with Max Verstappen; 18M in 1 month, now ~30–40M; designer Britney):
- **"Click to unpause."** Show a paused moment just before the action (the start line).
- Leading lines pull the eye to the action.
- Clean and uncomplicated.
- **Leave out the celebrity face** when the concept doesn't need it.
- Don't double up the same POV.
- **Podcast version:** the guest mid-speech plus a quote. A smiling, thumbs-up, posed shot is not click-to-unpause.
- **Everything is adaptation.** The concept came from CarWow's UK drag-race "lineup" shots.
- Mindset: top YouTubers are "**architects, engineers**" more than pure creatives. It is the same skill set as performance-marketing ad testing (host: "creative analyst").

## 9. Post-publish protocol: "When to panic" and the 1-of-10 expectation test (1:21:05–1:28:30)

1. **Before publishing, everyone predicts the video's rank** for the first hour, first day and first week.
   - "1 of 10" means the best of your last 10 uploads, as shown in YouTube Studio's ranking.
   - Ex.: an Elon-level guest is expected to be a 1/10.
2. **Judge performance against *expectation*, not channel average.**
   - Expected 1/10, actual 2/10: change the thumbnail. "It should be panic stations."
   - Expected 1/10, actual 4/10: **stop the A/B test and make a dramatic change.**
   - A video expected to be weak that lands 8/10 is fine. Don't panic.
   - Anecdote: one video did 4M views on day 1 and still counted as a failure.
3. **Let the A/B test pick the winning thumbnail** and set it as the main one. **Then try titles only if the video is still under expectation.**
4. **Data rules:**
   - **"Good data is better than no data"**: don't touch a video that is beating expectation.
   - **"No data is better than bad data"**: if it is underperforming, change it now rather than "give it more time."
5. **Don't change things constantly** (10 title changes on day 1). External factors such as suggested traffic from someone else's upload make it impossible to tell what worked. Make big changes only when performance is dramatically under expectation.

Also:
- It is sometimes rational to post a video you expect to underperform (for example, promoting your program) because of business upside. Do it rarely.
- The same expectation discipline applies to X posts (he averages ~200K views per tweet).
- "It's you versus you": the 1-of-10 game is about getting better than your past self.

## 10. Title principles (1:28:36–1:38:15)

1. **Fewest words that are still interesting.**
   - The old rule was under 50 characters. It is now looser because of TV display: **60+ characters is generally too long.**
2. **Universal language (beat the curse of knowledge).**
   - "D1 athlete" means nothing outside the US; write "pro/college athlete."
   - A "Goodwill" title did normally in the US but lost Asian and European viewers who didn't know the word.
   - "EBITDA" shrinks the TAM (Diary of a CEO says "profit/money"). Jargon is fine *only* if you deliberately target a qualified niche.
3. **Complement the thumbnail; don't repeat it.**
   - Title "How anyone can go viral in 2026" + thumbnail text "This is the key" works.
   - The same phrase on both does not.
4. **Readable at a glance:**
   - Short contemporary words ("modern" beats "contemporary").
   - No three-titles-mashed-together segmentation.
   - **Title Case** (capitalise each word except connectors).
   - **Avoid ALL CAPS**: uniform letter height makes it harder to read.
5. **Human interest over SEO (the most important rule).** Ignore "SEO optimizer" scores. Typical traffic shares:
   - **search ~5–10%** (rarely 50%+)
   - **subscription feed under 5%**
   - **channel page ~3%**
   - Example: "New 2026 iPhone 16 Review Unboxing Tutorial" loses to "**Don't Waste Your Money**" + iPhone 16, which still ranks.

**Live "good → better → best" title ladder (1:36:06):**
1. "The best value Porsche GT3 on the market". Generic.
2. "**I found** the best value Porsche GT3 on the market". "I found" adds a story.
3. "I found **the cheapest** 911 GT3 on the market". A superlative + a desirable object.
4. "I found the cheapest 911 GT3 on the market, **but nobody will buy it**". A **contradiction** creates human interest ("flooded? damaged?").
   - It is longer and scores worse on length rules, but it is far better.
   - Inverse pattern: "I found the worst apartment in NYC and it's expensive, but everyone wants to move in."
- Host: frameworks get you "0 → 80"; curiosity, taste and analytics get you "80 → 100."

## 11. Metrics: "The only two metrics he trusts" (1:38:19–1:43:38)

- **#1: Views.** It is a "comprehensive metric": click, watch and satisfaction are all baked in. "Blindfold me… all you have to do to grow a channel is look at the views."
- **#2: Retention curves**, the actual curve shape showing where people drop off. He has studied "thousands if not tens of thousands" of them.
- **CTR and AVD are misleading, though not irrelevant:**
  - **Lemonade stand:** ~20% conversion outside your house versus a tiny rate in Times Square, but more sales in Times Square. "Some of the biggest channels I've ever worked on have some of the lowest CTRs because they're operating in the biggest markets."
  - AVD rises just by making longer videos.
- **The retention curve drops as reach broadens** (new audiences are less engaged), but **the drop-off *moments* stay the same across core, casual and new viewers.** So fixes generalise.
- **Universal drop trigger:** ending language. "**At the end of the day…**" signals the end and causes a drop.

## 12. The 45-Second Intro Formula (1:43:38–1:52:45)

General principles: **under ~45 seconds**, simple language, intrigue, proof points. He has ghostwritten "~10,000 intros."

1. **Deliver on the promise.** The title and thumbnail are a promise, so reaffirm them in the **first ~10 seconds**, visually (stronger) or verbally.
   - He has seen **60–70% of viewers leave within 10 seconds** when this is missed.
   - Intent: "You made the right choice. Trust me. Look at this."
2. **Create intrigue.** Use interest-based language, not a statement list.
   - Weak: "in this video we cover X, Y, Z, let's go."
   - Better: "…and how it leads to this, and we're even going to break down…"
   - Open **many small hooks and curiosity gaps** the viewer needs answered.
   - Use **the least context possible and drip-feed the rest.** MrBeast style: "This is an FBI agent. He's got 24 hours to catch me. Go," with no name or bio.
   - Over-explaining (long guest intros) was the host's early mistake.
3. **Seamless flow.** "**The best intro doesn't feel like an intro. It just feels like the video started.**"
   - Use a connecting sentence ("…and we're going to start with this") instead of "anyway, let's get into it."

**Podcast application:**
- A sizzle "trailer" (the host uses a 45-second sizzle, then a ~105-second intro) works only with high production and genuinely great clips, as on Diary of a CEO.
- Modern Wisdom simply opens with a question.
- "Today we sat down with…" reels: get rid of them.
- As the show grows, move toward something shorter.

## 13. Stakes ("Storytelling is useless without stakes") (1:58:34–2:01:50)

- Great structure plus no stakes means nobody is invested.
- **Live demo (crumpled paper into a bin):**
  - Plain throw: nobody cares.
  - "The camera guy says I can't": a little more invested.
  - "If I make it, Tiger gets $1M": now it matters.
  - Stack more stakes on top.
- **Stake types:**
  - an **enemy/doubter** ("my ex said I couldn't get fit")
  - a **time limit**
  - a **reward**
  - a **punishment**
  - **FOMO / the cost of not paying attention** ("don't waste your money on the wrong iPhone")
- **The stake must fit the video.** For an informational video, use the *cost of not knowing*.
  - Wrong: "I'll get slimed if I can't explain this portfolio in 10 minutes."
  - Right: "The difference between this portfolio and the average one is over **$3M in retirement**."

## 14. How the algorithm behaves (1:52:45–1:58:01)

- **YouTube's edge is that it is an evergreen algorithm.** Your feed shows videos from 2 hours to 8 years old. Videos that hold people long and satisfy them become evergreen.
- **"It is always right behind the audience."** It promotes whatever people click, watch and are satisfied by.
  - "If everyone only clicked 6-hour black-screen videos titled 'video one', that's all that would be displayed."
- **TV is now dominant:** TV was ~15–20% of client traffic 5–6 years ago and is **50–60% today** (60–70% for some channels).
  - Longer videos win on TV because *viewers* want 30-minute videos on TV, not because YouTube favours length.
  - AVD has roughly doubled in 5 years.
- **Near-meritocracy.** Small creators get a test pool; if they don't earn clicks and watches, they're done. "If you can make a better video than me, you'll get more views than me."
- **Small creators' advantage:** flexibility, and no core audience to disappoint. AI is dissolving the editing and graphics gatekeepers.

## 15. The YouTube Pendulum (stage-based focus) (2:10:51–2:13:00)

1. **Stage 1: Watchable.** Video and audio quality just good enough to watch. ("A $400B public brand's channel was literally not watchable.")
2. **Stage 2: Ideas + packaging.** Once the video is "okay," focus shifts to idea, title and thumbnail.
   - "Look at the top performers, make the top 20 with your own twist."
   - Mastering this "takes a very long time. I still haven't mastered it."

## 22. Recommended production workflow (synthesis for the app; INFERRED from the numbers above)

Paddy gives **no explicit weekly calendar or upload cadence**. This is my own structuring of his stated stages and ratios into an app pipeline.

1. **Monthly / ongoing ideation bank.** Log outliers (≥3x) from your niche and adjacent niches.
   - Tag each idea Internal / External / Innovation and score its CCN fit.
   - Target ~100 raw ideas per video you plan to publish.
2. **Weekly study block (3–5 h).** Review the swipe-file playlists (outliers, titles, thumbnails, intros) and add notes.
3. **Elimination pass.** Run 4–5 yes/no gates (packageable, feasible, clears view target, 80% overlap or a deliberate 20% experiment, logline in 1 sentence). Warn if more than 30% or fewer than 2% of ideas survive.
4. **Idea slides for the top 10:** thumbnail sketch, 2–3 titles, skeleton, logline, then a "career-bet" vote.
5. **Pre-production packaging (10–20% of the time budget):**
   - at least 10 titles
   - thumbnail brief with ~5 concepts
   - stakes chosen (enemy / time limit / reward / punishment / cost of not knowing)
   - intro drafted with the 3-step formula, under 45 s
6. **Shoot:** video, plus 10–30 thumbnail photos per concept.
7. **Edit and design:** seed context for new viewers (CCN), cut ending-language drop triggers, and make at least 3 differentiated thumbnails.
8. **Publish:** pick the career-bet title, load 3 thumbnails into the A/B test, and **record the predicted 1-of-10 rank** for hour, day and week.
9. **Review (hour 1 / day 1 / week 1):** compare the actual rank with the prediction.
   - 3 or more ranks below expectation: stop the test and make a dramatic thumbnail change, then a title change.
   - Otherwise let the test finish.
10. **Post-mortem:** read the retention curve (find the drop moments), log the views, and feed winners back into the Internal idea pool for remakes.
11. **Stage gate (Pendulum):** watchable? → idea and packaging mastery → production upgrades.
